## Profession Identity

**Code:** `ST`

Scout progression is built around increasingly broad operational understanding of space, movement, clues, routes, and uncertainty.

The core progression is:

```text
ST1 — Observe, Navigate & Locate
"What is around us, where can we safely go, and what immediate signs matter?"

ST2 — Track & Predict
"What moved through here, where did it go, and where is it likely to be now?"

ST3 — Recon & Exploit
"What does the wider operational picture look like, and how can we use it?"
```

The Profession-wide principle is:

> Scout Curriculum supplies field observation, navigation, tracking, search, and reconnaissance method. Subject Theory supplies what the observed thing actually means.

A high-tier Scout does not automatically understand unknown alien technology, biology, culture, hazards, or motives.

## Core Profession Boundary

Scout answers:

```text
What is around us?
Where can we go?
What changed here?
What moved through here?
Where is it likely going?
What does the wider operational picture suggest?
What do we still need to know?
```

Scientist answers:

```text
What does this evidence scientifically establish?
What phenomenon is occurring?
What general explanation fits?
```

Technician answers:

```text
How does this mechanism work?
What failed?
How can it be repaired, bypassed, or modified?
```

Soldier answers:

```text
What does this mean tactically?
How should force be applied?
Which positions, targets, or combat actions matter?
```

The critical Scout principle is:

> Scout turns uncertain space into usable operational knowledge without pretending that observation equals understanding.

## Tier I — ST1 — Observe, Navigate & Locate

### Core Promise

> Systematically observe the immediate environment, maintain orientation, assess routes, identify meaningful clues and hazards, search local areas, and locate nearby access or movement signs.

Core question:

> **What is around us, where can we safely go, and what immediate signs matter?**

Shorthand:

```text
OBSERVE / NAVIGATE / LOCATE
```

ST1's defining principle is:

> ST1 converts the immediate unknown environment into reliable, bounded field knowledge.

### ST1 Certification

```text

2. Commission Scout Tools I
3. Environmental Observation
4. Route Assessment
5. Navigation & Orientation
6. Clue Recognition
7. Search & Location
8. Field Reporting & Verification
```


### FIELD_SAFETY_I

Supports recognizing ordinary known field hazards and unsafe movement conditions such as:

* unstable terrain;
* exposed heights;
* known environmental hazards;
* obvious structural instability;
* dangerous footing;
* known biological or chemical field hazards;
* ordinary movement risks;
* identifying when conditions exceed available capability.

Subject Theory remains mandatory.

```text
FIELD_SAFETY_I
+
known environmental Theory
=
meaningful hazard recognition
```

For something genuinely unknown, ST1 may establish only:

```text
UNUSUAL_ENVIRONMENTAL_CONDITION
POTENTIAL_HAZARD
```

rather than identifying the mechanism.

### ENVIRONMENTAL_OBSERVATION_I

Supports systematic observation of uncontrolled field environments for operationally useful information.

Potential outputs include:

* unusual objects or conditions;
* recent disturbance;
* movement signs;
* changes from prior visits;
* obvious points of interest;
* comparison between nearby areas;
* anomalous activity.

Example:

```text
Most dust is undisturbed.
The eastern passage has fresh footprints and displaced debris.
```

This does not automatically establish:

```text
Enemy patrol passed east.
```

That requires interpretation.

### FIELD_ORIENTATION_I

Supports determining current spatial relationship to:

* known landmarks;
* mapped terrain;
* mission objectives;
* explored space;
* return routes;
* visible references.

ST1 can:

* maintain direction;
* reorient after displacement;
* recognize uncertainty in position;
* relate current location to known map data;
* identify likely return direction.

This competency defines structured field method.

The universal Navigation stat remains the performance variable.

### ROUTE_ASSESSMENT_I

Supports evaluating an immediate route before committing the team.

Potential information includes:

```text
obstruction
terrain difficulty
vertical transition
likely traversal risk
obvious environmental hazard
special equipment need
route exposure
probable continuation
```

Example:

```text
North passage:
clear but exposed.

East passage:
partially collapsed.

Lower access:
possible with climbing equipment.
```

### NAVIGATION_I

Supports:

* following known routes;
* retracing paths;
* maintaining heading;
* choosing between understood route options;
* recognizing route deviation;
* navigating ordinary unfamiliar terrain;
* using maps and markers;
* coordinating return paths.

The distinction is:

```text
Navigation stat
=
how effectively the Unit performs navigation

NAVIGATION_I
=
trained field method permitting structured navigation interactions
```

A high-Navigation non-Scout may still perform ordinary movement well.

Scout should not be mandatory for basic exploration.

### CLUE_RECOGNITION_I

Supports recognizing that an observed field detail may be operationally meaningful.

Potential clue classes include:

```text
RECENT_MOVEMENT
DISTURBED_SURFACE
BROKEN_VEGETATION
FRESH_TOOL_MARKS
RECENTLY_OPENED
DISPLACED_OBJECT
FOOTPRINTS
DRAG_MARKS
SPENT_CASING
BLOOD_TRACE
RECENT_CAMP
IMPROVISED_MARKER
```

The Scout may identify:

> This is worth investigating.

That does not mean the Scout immediately knows the full explanation.

### FIELD_SEARCH_I

Supports deliberate local search for:

* operationally relevant signs;
* routes;
* hidden access;
* ordinary concealed objects;
* disturbed areas;
* movement traces;
* likely concealment points.

Critical principle:

> Search reveals detectable existing Reality. Search does not create hidden content.

### LOCATION_ASSESSMENT_I

Supports answering bounded local questions such as:

```text
Where did these tracks enter?
Which nearby doorway was recently used?
Where is that sound coming from?
Which visible route continues toward the signal?
Where is the safest immediate fallback position?
```

ST1 does not perform long-range predictive tracking.

That belongs to ST2.

### FIELD_HAZARD_RECOGNITION_I

Supports identifying ordinary immediate hazards such as:

```text
UNSTABLE_FLOOR
RECENT_COLLAPSE
KNOWN_PREDATOR_SIGN
FLOODED_PASSAGE
VISIBLE_TRIPWIRE
ACTIVE_FIRE
TOXIC_ENVIRONMENT_SIGN
UNSAFE_VERTICAL_DROP
```

Subject Theory still determines the exact meaning.

Example:

```text
Several small animals are dead near the doorway.
Potential environmental hazard.
```

The Scout may not know the actual toxin or cause.

### FIELD_DOCUMENTATION_I

Supports recording:

* routes;
* landmarks;
* hazards;
* observed clues;
* access points;
* searched locations;
* movement signs;
* map corrections;
* unresolved questions;
* known limitations.

Field Knowledge persists institutionally when successfully recorded.

Example:

```text
Reality:
Hidden service tunnel exists.

Knowledge:
SGC knows where the tunnel entrance is.
```

Later teams should not need to rediscover it unless the Reality itself changes.

### ROUTE_VERIFICATION_I

Supports checking whether known or proposed routes remain valid.

Potential outcomes include:

```text
route still passable
new obstruction
new hazard
marker missing
route changed
previous map stale
```

Example:

```text
Previously:
west corridor clear

Now:
structural collapse blocks west corridor
```

The old map was historically accurate.

Current Reality changed.

Scout updates the Knowledge state.

### ST1 Search Coverage Rule

ST1 exposes an important universal rule:

> Search coverage must be method-specific.

A tile should not simply become globally:

```text
SEARCHED
```

Instead the game needs to know what method has been applied.

Conceptually:

```yaml
searchRecord:
  subject:
    TILE_042

  method:
    FIELD_SEARCH_I

  coverage:
    GENERAL_FIELD_SEARCH

  result:
    completed

  revealed:
    - ACCESS_PANEL_03
```

Later:

```text
SUBSURFACE_SCAN
```

may reveal something that ordinary field search could not.

Implementation may not require a dedicated `SearchRecord` type, but the semantic distinction is required.

### ST1 Capability Boundary

ST1 can:

* systematically observe field environments;
* orient within known or interpretable space;
* assess immediate routes;
* navigate ordinary terrain;
* search areas;
* identify meaningful clues;
* recognize ordinary field hazards;
* locate nearby access;
* follow immediate signs;
* verify known routes;
* preserve field context;
* update maps.

ST1 does not inherently:

* predict distant movement;
* reconstruct complex movement history;
* identify unknown technology;
* determine unknown biological causes;
* reveal every hidden object;
* remove fog of war;
* know unobserved destinations;
* turn every clue into authoritative hidden truth.

## Tier II — ST2 — Track & Predict

### Core Promise

> Reconstruct movement from field Evidence, distinguish overlapping trails, infer likely destinations, and predict where a moving subject is most likely to go next.

Core question:

> **What moved through here, where did it go, and where is it likely to be now?**

Shorthand:

```text
TRACK / PREDICT
```

ST2's defining change is:

> ST2 connects separate pieces of field Evidence into movement history and bounded prediction.

```text

10. Detect Deception & False Trails
11. Conduct Extended Tracking
12. Verify Track Reconstruction

COMPLETE → ST2
```

### FIELD_SIGN_INTERPRETATION_II

Supports interpreting field signs as Evidence of movement, direction, pace, and recent activity.

Potential signs include:

* footprints;
* drag marks;
* broken vegetation;
* disturbed dust;
* displaced objects;
* open doors;
* discarded equipment;
* blood traces;
* vehicle marks;
* climbing signs;
* disturbed soil or water.

ST1:

```text
"These tracks are recent."
```

ST2:

```text
"One group entered from the south, paused here, then continued east."
```

### TRACK_IDENTIFICATION_II

Supports determining which field signs likely belong to the same subject or group.

Potential outcomes include:

```text
same trail
different trail
individual movement
group movement
group split
group merge
changed movement signature
```

Example:

```text
Tracks A:
heavy boots
regular stride
three individuals

Tracks B:
bare feet
irregular stride
one individual
```

ST2 may establish persistent Knowledge identifiers such as:

```text
TRACK_GROUP_A
TRACK_INDIVIDUAL_B
```

### MOVEMENT_RECONSTRUCTION_II

Supports reconstructing likely movement sequence.

Potential findings include:

* entry point;
* exit point;
* pauses;
* direction changes;
* pursuit;
* group splits;
* group merges;
* movement around obstacles;
* interactions with objects.

Example:

```text
1. Group entered through west door.
2. One person approached control panel.
3. Group moved north.
4. One individual returned alone.
5. Everyone later exited east.
```

This establishes movement history.

It does not automatically establish motive.

### TRAIL_DISCRIMINATION_II

Supports separating:

* old from new movement;
* several groups;
* crossing trails;
* contaminated signs;
* overlapping traffic;
* relevant movement from background traffic.

Example:

```text
Room contains:
old maintenance traffic
recent civilian movement
one very recent armed patrol
```

ST2 can identify distinct trail groups instead of treating all signs as one event.

### DIRECTION_TIMING_ESTIMATION_II

Supports estimating:

* direction of movement;
* rough age of signs;
* sequence;
* relative speed;
* lead distance;
* whether target is approaching or departing.

Estimates should remain bounded.

Good:

```text
Likely passed within the last hour.
```

Bad unless evidence genuinely supports it:

```text
Passed exactly 43 minutes ago.
```

### DESTINATION_INFERENCE_II

Supports inferring likely destination from:

* movement direction;
* route network;
* terrain;
* known locations;
* mission context;
* target constraints.

Example:

```text
Tracks move northeast.

Known terrain:
river crossing northeast
dead end east
collapsed route north

Inference:
river crossing is the most plausible destination.
```

This remains an interpretation, not guaranteed Reality.

### MOVEMENT_PREDICTION_II

Supports predicting:

* likely next tile;
* likely route;
* likely destination;
* likely intercept point;
* likely branch choice;
* whether target remains nearby.

ST1:

```text
"Tracks continue north."
```

ST2:

```text
"The group is moving north toward the only passable bridge.
The bridge is the most likely intercept point."
```

Critical boundary:

```text
PREDICTION
≠
REALITY
```

Predictions may be wrong without being irrational if based on valid but incomplete Evidence.

### DECEPTION_RECOGNITION_II

Supports recognizing:

* false trails;
* deliberate backtracking;
* track concealment;
* planted evidence;
* staged disturbance;
* misleading markers;
* route masking;
* decoy behavior.

ST2 may establish:

```text
"These tracks were deliberately created to suggest eastward movement."
```

It does not automatically establish the true route.

### EXTENDED_TRACKING_II

Supports:

* tracking across multiple tiles;
* maintaining trail identity;
* crossing terrain changes;
* reacquiring lost trails;
* comparing signs across locations;
* continuing after temporary gaps;
* identifying route changes.

This extends beyond ST1's immediate sign-following.

### Trail Reacquisition

A useful ST2 loop is:

```text
Trail lost
↓
last known direction
+
terrain
+
target movement pattern
+
likely destinations
↓
prioritize search locations
↓
attempt reacquisition
```

This avoids a binary:

```text
track visible
or
track gone forever
```

### SEARCH_PRIORITIZATION_II

Supports determining where additional search effort is most likely to produce useful Evidence.

Example:

```text
Trail lost at junction.

West:
open exposed corridor

North:
muddy service passage

East:
sealed door
```

ST2 may conclude:

```text
North has the highest chance of preserving useful movement signs.
```

This does not reveal whether the target actually went north.

It improves the player's next decision.

### TRACKING_DOCUMENTATION_II

Supports recording:

* trail identities;
* movement segments;
* direction;
* rough timing;
* movement sequence;
* evidence gaps;
* candidate destinations;
* predictions;
* false-trail findings.

This allows one team to continue another team's investigation.

Example:

```text
Known:
last confirmed location
likely movement direction
predicted destination
confidence limits
```

The original Scout does not need to remain present for institutional Knowledge to persist.

### RECONSTRUCTION_VERIFICATION_II

Supports:

* checking movement reconstruction against new Evidence;
* revising route sequence;
* detecting contradictions;
* separating confirmed from inferred segments;
* rejecting unsupported paths;
* updating predictions.

Conceptually:

```text
CONFIRMED_SEGMENT
INFERRED_SEGMENT
UNKNOWN_SEGMENT
```

may all coexist in one reconstruction.

### ST2 Knowledge Authority

ST2 normally originates:

```text
OBSERVATION
EVIDENCE
INTERPRETATION
QUESTION
INSTANCE_FINDING

```

Scout-specific semantics may include:

### ST2 and Scientific Hypothesis

Scout should not force every possible route explanation into a scientific `HYPOTHESIS` record.

Most tracking reasoning can use:

```text
INTERPRETATION
PREDICTION
INSTANCE_FINDING
QUESTION
```

That is sufficient for operational reasoning.

### ST2 Combat Boundary

Scout may contribute:

```text
likely approach direction
recent hostile passage
probable patrol route
likely withdrawal route
possible ambush location
```

Soldier owns:

```text
firing position
assault plan
target priority
suppression
combat maneuver
```

Scout provides movement knowledge.

Soldier converts it into combat tactics.

### ST2 Capability Boundary

ST2 can:

* interpret movement signs;
* associate trails;
* reconstruct movement;
* distinguish overlapping movement;
* estimate direction and rough timing;
* infer destinations;
* predict likely movement;
* detect false trails;
* maintain extended pursuit;
* reacquire trails;
* prioritize search;
* verify movement reconstruction.

ST2 does not inherently:

* reveal exact target location;
* identify unknown actors;
* know motives without Evidence;
* defeat all deception;
* provide combat command;
* remove fog of war;
* turn predictions into guaranteed truth.

## Tier III — ST3 — Recon & Exploit

### Core Promise

> Build a broader operational picture from incomplete field information, identify important information gaps, understand route and movement relationships, and expose useful mission opportunities.

Core question:

> **What does the wider operational picture look like, what are we still missing, and how can we use what we know?**

Shorthand:

```text
RECON / EXPLOIT
```

ST3's defining change is:

> ST3 combines multiple field Knowledge sources into a coherent operational picture and identifies better ways to approach the mission.

### ST3 Certification

```text
1. Senior Field Operations Examination
2. Commission Scout Tools III
3. Integrate Multi-Source Reconnaissance
4. Establish Operational Picture
5. Identify Information Gaps
6. Develop Reconnaissance Plan
7. Conduct Remote & Indirect Reconnaissance
8. Analyze Route Networks
9. Identify Operational Opportunities
10. Assess Counter-Reconnaissance
11. Develop Search & Intercept Strategy
12. Validate Reconnaissance Picture

COMPLETE → ST3
```

### ST3 Competencies

```text
RECONNAISSANCE_SYNTHESIS_III
```

### RECONNAISSANCE_SYNTHESIS_III

Supports combining:

```text
Scout observations
Track reconstructions
Map Knowledge
Known routes
Remote sensor reports
Witness reports
Enemy sightings
Environmental conditions
Mission objectives
Known hazards
Other Profession findings
```

Example:

```text
ST1:
Service tunnel found.

ST2:
Recent movement through tunnel.

SO:
Spent casings indicate organized fighting nearby.

Power was restored recently in the eastern wing.

Mission:
Locate surviving facility personnel.
```

ST3 may synthesize:

```text
Eastern wing is likely still occupied,
and the service tunnel is probably an active access route.
```

This is an operational interpretation, not hidden truth.

### Scout III Uses Other Professions' Knowledge

ST3 can use findings generated by other Professions.

It does not gain those Professions' methods.

Example:

```text
TE finding:
GENERATOR_RECENTLY_REPAIRED

ME finding:
BLOOD_TRACE_IS_HUMAN

SO finding:
FIRING_POSITION_RECENTLY_USED
```

ST3 may combine them spatially.

It cannot independently perform the technical, medical, or tactical analysis that originally established them.

### OPERATIONAL_PICTURE_DEVELOPMENT_III

Supports organizing what is:

```text

inferred
predicted
unknown
stale
unverified
```

Potential contents include:

```text
unsearched areas
```

Critical rule:

> Operational Picture is a composed view over Knowledge, not another authority layer.

Reality remains authoritative.

### INFORMATION_GAP_ANALYSIS_III

Supports identifying which missing piece of information most limits an operational decision.

Examples:

```text
Which of two exits did the target use?

What is the patrol interval?

Is the lower bridge intact?

Which entrance is currently active?

Where does the underground route emerge?
```

ST3 can raise focused operational Questions.

This creates a reconnaissance loop:

```text

↓

↓
Reconnaissance Question
↓
Choose method
↓
Gather Evidence
↓
Update picture
```

This mirrors scientific inquiry structurally without turning Scout into Scientist.

### RECONNAISSANCE_PLANNING_III

Supports determining how to obtain missing field information.

Potential considerations include:

* observation location;
* search route;
* sensor placement;
* area priority;
* information dependency;
* direct versus remote observation;

* alternate methods.

Conceptually:

```text

Reconnaissance Plan
```

### Scout vs Soldier on Chokepoints

Scout may establish:


```text
Junction 4 is a strong defensive position.
```

Scout owns the spatial relationship.

### OPERATIONAL_OPPORTUNITY_ANALYSIS_III

Supports identifying useful opportunities created by current Reality and Knowledge.

Possible opportunities include:

```text
alternate route
intercept point

```

Example:

```text

```

The Scout exposes the opportunity.

The player or another Profession determines how to use it.

### Scout Exploitation vs Soldier Tactics

Scout:

```text
"There is a two-minute gap in their patrol coverage
and an alternate route behind storage."
```

Soldier:

```text
"We can use that gap to breach from two directions
and block their withdrawal."
```

Scout shapes available options.

Soldier shapes combat execution.

### COUNTER_RECONNAISSANCE_III

* hostile surveillance;
* deliberate trail cleaning;
* sensor placement;
* false route markers;
* staged bait;
* repeated counter-tracking;
* evidence destruction;
* controlled movement intended to obscure destination.


### Counter-Reconnaissance Boundary

### SEARCH_STRATEGY_III

```text

travel time
```

Example:


ST3 may conclude:


* invalidate old predictions;
* flag changed routes;
* recognize missing coverage;
* revise search priority;
* distinguish old observation from current confirmation.

This makes Knowledge freshness important.

## Scout and Perception

Scout does not monopolize the universal Perception stat.

All Units can perceive.

The distinction is:

```text
Perception stat
=
how effectively a Unit notices detectable information

Scout competency
=
what the Unit knows to look for,
how they search,
and what operational interpretations they can make
```

Example:

```text

notices footprint

```

## Scout and Navigation

Likewise:

```text
Navigation stat
=
performance variable

Scout navigation competency
=
trained field method and access to richer navigation interactions
```

A Scout should improve exploration quality.

A Scout should not be mandatory for basic movement.

## Cross-Profession Boundaries

### Soldier Boundary

Scout commonly establishes:

```text
where movement occurred
which route is exposed
where patrols travel
where routes converge
which access appears unwatched
where contact is likely
```

Soldier commonly establishes:

```text
best firing position
assault direction
target priority
defensive value
suppression plan
combat maneuver
```

The useful handoff is:

```text
Scout:
"They pass this junction every six minutes."

Soldier:
"Then this is where we set the ambush."
```

### Technician Boundary

Scout:

```text
"This panel has been opened recently."
```

Technician:

```text
"The regulator was deliberately bypassed."
```

Scout locates and contextualizes.

Technician interprets technical function.

### Scientist Boundary

Scout:

```text
"Three sets of tracks crossed this corridor recently.
One continues east."
```

Scientist:

```text
"The residue in one track contains a compound not found in the others."
```

Scout reasons about operational movement in uncontrolled environments.

Scientist reasons about phenomena through controlled Evidence and scientific method.

Base Tier III remains ordinary senior Profession competency. Specialization territory remains distinct and must not be absorbed into the base curriculum.

## Final Profession Standard

A Scout should never function as a generic Perception bonus.

The Profession should create gameplay by answering progressively broader operational questions:

```text
ST1:
What is here, where can we go, and what immediate sign matters?

ST2:
What moved through here, where did it go, and where is it likely going?

ST3:
What does the wider operational picture tell us,
what are we still missing,
and what useful mission options does that create?
```

The Scout's power is therefore not:

```text
more vision
```

It is:

```text
better field Knowledge
+
better navigation
+
better search
+
better reconstruction
+
better prediction
+
better reconnaissance decisions
```
