## Profession Identity

**Code:** `SO`

Soldier progression is built around increasingly broad combat judgment, from surviving the immediate threat, to shaping the engagement, to directing combat effort toward the mission objective.

The core progression is:

```text
SO1 — Fight & Protect
"What is the immediate threat and how do I fight it?"

SO2 — Control & Maneuver
"How do we shape this engagement to our advantage?"

SO3 — Assess & Direct
"What is the larger tactical situation and how should our available force be used?"
```

The Profession-wide principle is:

> Soldier Curriculum supplies combat method, tactical judgment, force application, and ordinary combat leadership. Subject Theory supplies what weapons, enemies, defenses, hazards, and behaviors actually mean.

A high-tier Soldier does not automatically understand unknown alien weapons, biology, technology, motives, doctrine, or culture.

## Core Profession Boundary

Soldier answers:

```text
What is the immediate combat threat?
How should force be applied?
Which routes or positions matter during this engagement?
How can movement be controlled?
What is the decisive tactical problem?
What must be protected or controlled?
When should we escalate, withdraw, or stop fighting?
```

Scout answers:

```text
Where did they move?
Where are they likely going?
Which routes connect the area?
What is known or unknown about the wider environment?
```

Technician answers:

```text
How does the weapon or defensive system work?
What failed?
How can it be repaired, disabled, modified, or bypassed?
```

Scientist answers:

```text
What phenomenon explains this combat behavior?
Why does the weapon, shield, organism, or material behave this way?
```

Diplomat answers:

```text
What does the other party want?
What does surrender or negotiation mean culturally?
What terms, authority, or leverage can change their voluntary behavior?
```

The critical Soldier principle is:

> Soldier turns known combat conditions into better force decisions without replacing the systems that establish physical truth, intent, movement, or technical mechanism.

## Tier I — SO1 — Fight & Protect

### Core Promise

> Apply trained force safely and effectively, recognize immediate combat threats, protect others, and respond correctly to ordinary tactical situations.

Core question:

> **What is the immediate threat, and how do I fight it without unnecessarily exposing the team?**

Shorthand:

```text
FIGHT / PROTECT
```

SO1's defining principle is:

> Recognize the immediate threat, fight effectively, protect the team, and know when the fight is over.

### SO1 Certification

```text
1. Combat Foundations Examination
2. Commission Soldier Tools I
3. Weapon Handling & Employment
4. Immediate Threat Assessment
5. Defensive Action
6. Combat Movement
7. Protective Action
8. Engagement Resolution & Security

COMPLETE → SO1
```

### SO1 Competencies

```text
COMBAT_SAFETY_I
WEAPON_EMPLOYMENT_I
IMMEDIATE_THREAT_ASSESSMENT_I

DEFENSIVE_ACTION_I
COMBAT_MOVEMENT_I
PROTECTIVE_ACTION_I

ENGAGEMENT_DISCIPLINE_I
HOSTILE_CAPABILITY_RECOGNITION_I
COMBAT_OBSERVATION_I

SURRENDER_RECOGNITION_I
POST_ENGAGEMENT_SECURITY_I
COMBAT_HANDOFF_I
```

### COMBAT_SAFETY_I

Supports ordinary safe handling and employment of known weapons and combat equipment.

Includes:

* weapon safety;
* friendly-fire awareness;
* known hazardous firing conditions;
* ammunition handling;
* safe weapon state;
* ordinary firing restrictions;
* recognition of unsafe engagement conditions;
* known explosive or energy-weapon precautions where Theory permits.

Subject Theory remains mandatory.

```text
Soldier competency
+
subject Theory
=
valid combat understanding
```

A Soldier familiar with conventional weapons does not automatically know whether firing an unknown alien device in a shielded reactor environment is safe.

### WEAPON_EMPLOYMENT_I

Supports ordinary competent use of known personal combat weapons.

Conceptually:

```text
Weapon capability
+
required weapon knowledge
+
WEAPON_EMPLOYMENT_I
+
current state
=
valid attack interaction
```

The weapon determines:

```text
range
damage behavior
ammunition or resource use
attack type
special effects
valid targets
```

Soldier provides the trained method.

### IMMEDIATE_THREAT_ASSESSMENT_I

Supports determining what currently presents an immediate combat threat.

Potential factors include:

```text
armed actor
active attack
hostile movement
weapon orientation
dangerous creature behavior
active turret
imminent charge
known explosive threat
```

Potential findings may include:

```text
ACTIVE_THREAT
IMMEDIATE_THREAT
POTENTIAL_THREAT
NON_THREAT
THREAT_STATUS_UNCLEAR
```

Threat must be grounded in observable Reality and known behavior.

### DEFENSIVE_ACTION_I

Supports ordinary combat actions intended primarily to reduce incoming harm.

Potential methods include:

```text
TAKE_DEFENSIVE_POSITION_I
BREAK_LINE_OF_FIRE_I
DEFENSIVE_WITHDRAWAL_I
PROTECTIVE_POSITIONING_I
```

Exact Resolution Type IDs can be authored later.

Combat does not require a universal tile-cover system.

Encounter and Theory state can expose meaningful defensive relationships instead.

### COMBAT_MOVEMENT_I

Supports movement while under immediate hostile pressure.

Examples:

* advance under threat;
* withdraw safely;
* cross exposed space;
* reposition around an enemy;
* move toward an objective while engaged;
* maintain combat readiness while moving;
* avoid obstructing allies.

This is different from Scout Navigation.

```text
Scout:
How do we reach the facility?

Soldier:
How do I cross this room while somebody is shooting at me?
```

### PROTECTIVE_ACTION_I

Supports ordinary protection of another Unit during combat.

Examples:

```text
assist endangered Unit
cover withdrawal
protect casualty evacuation
position against an immediate threat
help vulnerable Unit leave danger
```

This must remain below Guardian specialization territory.

Base Soldier provides competent ordinary protection.

Guardian owns exceptional interception and protection effects.

### ENGAGEMENT_DISCIPLINE_I

Supports recognizing when force should:

```text
begin
continue
change
stop
```

based on engagement state.

Relevant situations include:

```text
target incapacitated
target surrendered
target disengaging
friendly crossed firing path
civilian entered engagement
objective condition changed
```

Soldiers should not mechanically behave as kill-everything automatons.

### HOSTILE_CAPABILITY_RECOGNITION_I

Supports recognizing ordinary known combat capabilities from observed behavior.

Examples:

```text
ranged weapon
melee threat
shielded target
armored target
explosive weapon
rapid-fire weapon
known creature attack
fixed defensive weapon
```

For unfamiliar technology, Soldier may know only:

```text
Target emits directed energy attack.
```

rather than identifying the actual mechanism.

### COMBAT_OBSERVATION_I

Supports recording combat-relevant observations such as:

```text
weapon fired three times before reload
shield failed after sustained attack
creature retreated after injury
turret tracks movement
enemy avoided illuminated area
target protected another Unit
```

Combat therefore becomes a valid source of Evidence.

### SURRENDER_RECOGNITION_I

Supports recognizing established surrender behavior where cultural knowledge permits.

For familiar humans:

```text
weapon dropped
hands raised
verbal surrender
ceasing resistance
```

may be sufficient.

For unfamiliar species:

```text
gesture meaning unknown
```

may require Diplomat or cultural Theory.

### POST_ENGAGEMENT_SECURITY_I

Supports ordinary immediate actions after active fighting stops:

```text
verify immediate threat ended
secure known weapons
identify incapacitated persons
identify surrendering persons
identify immediate hazards
maintain basic scene security
prepare casualty handoff
prepare prisoner handoff
```

Soldier secures the immediate scene.

The custody system determines longer-term detention.

### COMBAT_HANDOFF_I

Supports preserving information and transferring responsibility.

Examples:

```text
casualties → Medic
captured equipment → Receiving
prisoners → Security
technical hazards → Technician
unknown phenomena → Scientist
witnesses → Diplomat
movement questions → Scout
```

Field observations remain valid Evidence after handoff.

### SO1 Knowledge Authority

SO1 normally originates:

```text
OBSERVATION
EVIDENCE
INTERPRETATION
QUESTION
INSTANCE_FINDING
```

Soldier-flavored findings may include:

```text
IMMEDIATE_THREAT_FINDING
HOSTILE_CAPABILITY_FINDING
ENGAGEMENT_STATE_FINDING
COMBAT_BEHAVIOR_FINDING
SURRENDER_FINDING
SECURITY_FINDING
```

SO1 does not normally originate generalized Theory-level knowledge.

### SO1 Capability Boundary

SO1 can:

* safely employ known weapons;
* recognize immediate known combat threats;
* fight ordinary engagements;
* reposition under hostile pressure;
* take ordinary defensive action;
* protect another Unit through ordinary combat action;
* recognize known hostile capabilities;
* observe enemy combat behavior;
* recognize familiar surrender behavior;
* stop applying force when the threat ends;
* secure the immediate scene;
* preserve combat Evidence.

SO1 does not inherently:

* command the whole team;
* create advanced tactical plans;
* understand unknown weapon mechanisms;
* provide exceptional precision attacks;
* guarantee protection;
* automatically act before enemies;
* negotiate surrender terms.

## Tier II — SO2 — Control & Maneuver

### Core Promise

> Shape an engagement by controlling movement, pressure, exposure, and positioning rather than simply reacting to the nearest threat.

Core question:

> **How do we make the fight happen on better terms for us?**

Shorthand:

```text
CONTROL / MANEUVER
```

SO2's defining change is:

> SO2 moves from handling the immediate engagement to manipulating the structure of the fight.

### SO2 Certification

```text
1. Advanced Combat Examination
2. Commission Soldier Tools II
3. Assess Engagement Geometry
4. Establish Fire & Movement Control
5. Apply Suppression & Pressure
6. Conduct Coordinated Maneuver
7. Control Enemy Movement
8. Execute Fighting Withdrawal
9. Exploit Combat Openings
10. Counter Enemy Maneuver
11. Manage Multi-Threat Engagement
12. Validate Engagement Control

COMPLETE → SO2
```

### SO2 Competencies

```text
ENGAGEMENT_GEOMETRY_II
FIRE_MOVEMENT_COORDINATION_II
SUPPRESSION_II
COMBAT_PRESSURE_II

MANEUVER_COORDINATION_II
ENEMY_MOVEMENT_CONTROL_II
FIGHTING_WITHDRAWAL_II

COMBAT_OPENING_RECOGNITION_II
COUNTER_MANEUVER_II
MULTI_THREAT_MANAGEMENT_II

ENGAGEMENT_CONTROL_ASSESSMENT_II
TACTICAL_REASSESSMENT_II
```

### ENGAGEMENT_GEOMETRY_II

Supports understanding how physical arrangement affects combat.

Relevant factors include:

```text
movement lanes
exposed approaches
route convergence
fallback routes
blocked movement
objective access
escape routes
crossfire relationships
areas where force can be concentrated
```

Scout may establish:

```text
Three routes connect the east and west rooms.
```

SO2 may establish:

```text
If the center junction is held,
the eastern group cannot reach the objective without crossing it.
```

Scout understands spatial relationships.

Soldier understands their combat consequence.

### Combat Geometry Without Universal Cover

Combat should not require:

```text
HALF_COVER
FULL_COVER
```

as universal geometry.

Instead encounters can expose semantic relationships such as:

```text
EXPOSED_APPROACH
NARROW_ACCESS
BLOCKED_FIRE_ARC
DEFENSIBLE_ENTRY
LIMITED_RETREAT
OPEN_CROSSING
PROTECTED_WITHDRAWAL_ROUTE
```

SO2 reasons about those relationships.

### FIRE_MOVEMENT_COORDINATION_II

Supports ordinary coordination where one combat action enables another.

Examples:

```text
one Unit fires while another crosses
one Unit holds attention while another repositions
team advances in controlled sequence
team withdraws in stages
```

This remains below Tactician specialization.

Base SO2 provides ordinary trained coordination.

Tactician owns exceptional initiative and coordination benefits.

### SUPPRESSION_II

Supports using force to restrict an opponent's safe or desirable actions.

Possible consequences may include:

```text
SUPPRESSED
MOVEMENT_RESTRICTED
EXPOSED_IF_MOVING
FORCED_DELAY
REDUCED_ATTACK_FREEDOM
```

where weapon and target Theory support them.

Conceptually:

```text
weapon supports suppression
+
SUPPRESSION_II
+
valid target/context
=
suppression interaction
```

Suppression is not granted universally by Soldier rank.

### Suppression Is Not Mind Control

Different enemies may respond differently.

Examples:

```text
trained human:
holds position

panicked civilian:
flees unpredictably

armored drone:
ignores ineffective small-arms suppression

predator:
retreats from sustained fire

fanatic:
continues despite risk
```

Suppression changes the tactical cost of action.

It does not universally dictate behavior.

### COMBAT_PRESSURE_II

Supports sustained threat intended to force worse choices.

Potential uses include:

* force enemy away from objective;
* make a route unattractive;
* interrupt preparation;
* deny comfortable repositioning;
* create temporary opportunities;
* reduce time available to act.

Prefer concrete semantic consequences such as:

```text
ROUTE_CONTESTED
POSITION_UNSAFE
REPOSITION_REQUIRED
ATTACK_PREPARATION_INTERRUPTED
WITHDRAWAL_PRESSURED
```

rather than vague hidden pressure bonuses.

### MANEUVER_COORDINATION_II

Supports ordinary team repositioning around threats and objectives.

Examples:

* advance by alternate route;
* shift pressure to another approach;
* reposition around fixed defenses;
* support casualty evacuation;
* reposition toward objective;
* move around a blocked route.

Scout may identify the route.

SO2 determines how to use it during active combat.

### ENEMY_MOVEMENT_CONTROL_II

Supports using combat capability and geometry to restrict enemy movement.

Potential outcomes include:

```text
ROUTE_DENIED
RETREAT_ROUTE_CONTESTED
ADVANCE_BLOCKED
MOVEMENT_CHANNELLED
OBJECTIVE_ACCESS_CONTESTED
```

These outcomes depend on:

```text
weapon capability
position
target behavior
terrain
engagement state
```

SO2 cannot simply declare an enemy unable to move.

### Channeling

A useful SO2 concept is:

> Make one route sufficiently dangerous or unattractive that another route becomes more likely.

Example:

```text
Main corridor:
suppressed

Service exit:
open

Likely enemy response:
movement shifts toward service exit
```

This composes strongly with Scout Knowledge.

```text
Scout:
Service exit leads to lower courtyard.

Soldier:
Pressure the main corridor and make the courtyard route their better option.
```

### FIGHTING_WITHDRAWAL_II

Supports disengaging while preserving enough combat control to reduce pursuit risk.

Potential methods include:

```text
staged withdrawal
rear pressure
route protection
casualty-supported withdrawal
withdrawal through contested space
```

Potential outcomes include:

```text
ORDERLY_WITHDRAWAL
PURSUIT_DELAYED
RETREAT_ROUTE_HELD
DISENGAGEMENT_WINDOW
```

Retreat is a valid tactical outcome.

It is not automatically equivalent to failure.

### COMBAT_OPENING_RECOGNITION_II

Supports recognizing temporary opportunities such as:

```text
enemy weapon reloading
shield temporarily down
enemy overextended
route briefly uncovered
enemy attention committed elsewhere
fixed weapon unable to track angle
retreat formation disrupted
```

SO1 can observe the condition.

SO2 identifies it as an actionable combat opening.

### Opening Recognition vs Marksman

SO2 can establish:

```text
Their shield drops briefly after firing.
Attack during that interval.
```

Marksman specialization should own exceptional offensive exploitation of such openings.

Base SO2 recognizes opportunities.

Marksman turns them into superior offensive effects.

### COUNTER_MANEUVER_II

Supports recognizing and responding to enemy attempts to reshape the engagement.

Examples:

```text
flanking
encirclement
route denial
disengagement
reinforcement
objective rush
diversion
pressure shift
```

Scout may identify movement.

SO2 determines its immediate tactical consequence.

### MULTI_THREAT_MANAGEMENT_II

Supports prioritizing attention when several threats are simultaneously relevant.

Relevant factors may include:

```text
immediate lethality
objective risk
route control
vulnerable allies
enemy capability
time pressure
```

The nearest enemy is not necessarily the most important combat problem.

### Tactical Priority Is Not Automatic Targeting

SO2 may establish:

```text
Enemy at Gate controls our withdrawal route.
```

That does not automatically command every allied Unit to attack that enemy.

Knowledge remains separate from order and execution.

### ENGAGEMENT_CONTROL_ASSESSMENT_II

Supports determining who currently controls important parts of the engagement.

Potential findings include:

```text
ROUTE_CONTROLLED_BY_FRIENDLY
ROUTE_CONTROLLED_BY_HOSTILE
ROUTE_CONTESTED
OBJECTIVE_ACCESS_CONTESTED
WITHDRAWAL_ROUTE_SECURE
WITHDRAWAL_ROUTE_THREATENED
FRIENDLY_POSITION_ISOLATED
```

These are volatile Instance Findings.

### TACTICAL_REASSESSMENT_II

Supports recognizing when previous combat assumptions are no longer valid.

Possible triggers include:

```text
new threat
friendly casualty
objective movement
route blocked
enemy withdrawal
new weapon capability revealed
environment change
reinforcement
surrender
```

SO2 may establish:

```text
hold position no longer viable
withdrawal route now threatened
original target no longer priority
engagement can safely disengage
```

### SO2 Knowledge Authority

SO2 normally originates:

```text
OBSERVATION
EVIDENCE
INTERPRETATION
QUESTION
INSTANCE_FINDING
```

and may create bounded short-horizon tactical predictions.

Soldier-flavored findings may include:

```text
ENGAGEMENT_GEOMETRY_FINDING
ROUTE_CONTROL_FINDING
THREAT_PRIORITY_FINDING
COMBAT_OPENING_FINDING
MANEUVER_FINDING
WITHDRAWAL_FINDING
TACTICAL_RISK_FINDING
```

### SO2 Capability Boundary

SO2 can:

* interpret engagement geometry;
* coordinate ordinary fire and movement;
* apply suppression with appropriate weapons;
* maintain combat pressure;
* coordinate maneuver;
* contest or deny routes;
* influence enemy movement;
* conduct fighting withdrawals;
* recognize temporary combat openings;
* counter enemy maneuver;
* prioritize among several threats;
* assess engagement control;
* reassess when conditions change.

SO2 does not inherently:

* command an entire force;
* guarantee enemy movement;
* suppress targets that the method cannot meaningfully affect;
* create nonexistent routes;
* provide exceptional initiative;
* provide exceptional precision attacks;
* guarantee ally protection;
* replace Scout reconnaissance.

## Tier III — SO3 — Assess & Direct

### Core Promise

> Integrate the whole active engagement, identify the decisive tactical problems, define what must be controlled or protected, and direct ordinary combat effort toward those priorities.

Core question:

> **What matters most in this fight, and how should our available force be used to achieve the objective?**

Shorthand:

```text
ASSESS / DIRECT
```

SO3's defining change is:

> SO3 stops thinking primarily about one threat or one maneuver and starts integrating the entire active engagement into a tactical picture with priorities, dependencies, and force requirements.

### SO3 Certification

```text
1. Senior Combat Examination
2. Commission Soldier Tools III
3. Synthesize Tactical Situation
4. Identify Decisive Tactical Factors
5. Establish Combat Priorities
6. Define Tactical Requirements
7. Coordinate Multiple Combat Elements
8. Manage Objective-Centered Engagement
9. Direct Reinforcement & Reserve Use
10. Assess Escalation & Disengagement
11. Adapt Force Employment
12. Validate Tactical Outcome

COMPLETE → SO3
```

### SO3 Competencies

```text
TACTICAL_SYNTHESIS_III
DECISIVE_FACTOR_ANALYSIS_III
COMBAT_PRIORITY_III

TACTICAL_REQUIREMENTS_III
MULTI_ELEMENT_COORDINATION_III

REINFORCEMENT_EMPLOYMENT_III

```

### TACTICAL_SYNTHESIS_III

Supports combining all relevant combat Knowledge into one tactical interpretation.

Inputs may include:

```text
enemy locations
known capabilities
friendly condition
route control
objective state
reinforcement status
withdrawal options
terrain relationships
hazards
casualties
ammo or resource state
Scout reconnaissance
Technician findings
Medic findings
mission constraints
```

SO3 uses these to answer:

> What actually matters in this engagement?

### Tactical Picture vs Scout Operational Picture

Scout operational picture asks:

```text
What is known about the environment, routes, movement, and uncertainty?
```

Soldier tactical picture asks:

```text
Given what we know, what matters for this active fight?
```

Scout may establish:

```text
Three routes lead to the Gate.
```

SO3 may establish:

```text
Only one matters because the artifact carrier cannot use
the vertical route and the eastern route is compromised.
```

### DECISIVE_FACTOR_ANALYSIS_III

Supports determining which current condition is most likely to decide the engagement.

Potential decisive factors include:

```text
route control
survival of a key Unit
objective protection
enemy heavy weapon
reinforcement arrival
withdrawal route
environmental hazard
ammunition scarcity
Gate timing
hostage safety
defensive-system status
```

SO2 asks:

> Which threat matters most right now?

SO3 asks:

> Which factor decides whether the mission objective succeeds?

### Decisive Factor Is Objective-Dependent

Example:

```text
Enemy heavy gun:
high lethality

Enemy scout:
low lethality

Mission:
prevent alarm transmission
```

The scout may be the decisive tactical problem if they are reaching the alarm.

Combat significance is not identical to raw lethality.

### COMBAT_PRIORITY_III

Supports establishing an ordered set of current tactical priorities.

Example:

```text
1. Protect hostage.
2. Prevent access to Gate controls.
3. Maintain withdrawal route.
4. Neutralize heavy weapon.
5. Secure prisoners if feasible.
```

Priorities remain Knowledge.

They are not executable orders.

### Priority Can Change

Example:

```text
Initial:
hold junction

Then:
hostage enters crossfire

New priority:
protect hostage

Then:
Gate destabilizes

New priority:
extract immediately
```

SO3 must revise tactical priorities as Reality changes.

### TACTICAL_REQUIREMENTS_III

Supports defining what conditions must be achieved or maintained for the tactical objective to remain viable.

Example:

```text
Objective:
Extract artifact.

Tactical Requirements:
- western Gate route remains usable
- artifact carrier remains mobile
- enemy heavy weapon cannot cover Gate approach
- at least one combat-capable Unit protects carrier
- Gate room must not be overrun before extraction
```

This produces structured operational Knowledge:

```text
TACTICAL_REQUIREMENTS
```

### MULTI_ELEMENT_COORDINATION_III

Supports organizing ordinary combat roles across several friendly elements.

Potential elements include:

```text
frontline combatant
artifact carrier
casualty escort
rear security
route-control element
response team
reinforcement group
```

SO3 may establish:

```text
Element A:
hold junction

Element B:
move casualty toward Gate

Element C:
delay reinforcement
```

This does not violate the one-Actor bubble contract.

The coordination exists at a higher semantic level and resolves into individual actions.

### Multi-Element Coordination vs Tactician

Base SO3:

> Can understand and organize ordinary multi-element combat activity.

Tactician specialization:

> Makes coordination, initiative, tempo, and command exceptionally effective.

Base SO3 should not automatically grant:

```text
all allies act first
free extra actions
global initiative advantage
perfect coordination
```

Those remain specialization territory.

### OBJECTIVE_CENTERED_COMBAT_III

Supports applying force in service of the actual mission objective.

Relevant objectives may include:

```text
hold
extract
rescue
delay
capture
protect
break contact
disable
secure
escort
deny access
survive until condition
```

Combat should not assume that eliminating all enemies is always the correct objective.

### Combat Victory Is Not Always Enemy Elimination

Valid successful outcomes may include:

```text
objective secured
enemy remains active
team disengages
mission succeeds
```

or:

```text
hostages rescued
enemy still controls facility
mission succeeds
```

or:

```text
enemy delayed long enough
civilian evacuation completed
mission succeeds
```

SO3 is the tier that most clearly reasons this way.

### REINFORCEMENT_EMPLOYMENT_III

Supports determining where incoming friendly combat capability is most useful.

Possible recommendations include:

```text
reinforce Gate room
support casualty evacuation
contest side route
replace isolated frontline Unit
hold reinforcement until threat direction known
```

This does not bypass allocation or dispatch rules.

Reinforcements remain real Actors with:

```text
availability
travel time
assignment
Base Response state
```

### RESERVE_EMPLOYMENT_III

Supports deciding whether to keep some combat capability uncommitted.

Potential reasons include:

```text
protect unknown approach
respond to breakthrough
replace casualties
counter reinforcement
secure extraction
avoid committing to decoy
```

This is a tactical concept, not a new scheduling system.

### Tactical Reserve vs Base Response

These remain distinct:

```text
TACTICAL RESERVE
=
combat element intentionally held back within the current operation

BASE RESPONSE
=
dedicated base-level assignment removing Units from ordinary availability
```

### ESCALATION_ASSESSMENT_III

Supports deciding whether increasing force is tactically useful or dangerous.

Potential factors include:

```text
enemy reinforcement
civilian presence
unknown weapon
structural risk
hostage proximity
mission objective
available alternatives
```

Potential findings include:

```text
ESCALATION_REQUIRED
ESCALATION_UNNECESSARY
ESCALATION_RISKY
CURRENT_FORCE_SUFFICIENT
DISENGAGEMENT_PREFERRED
```

Tactical effectiveness and organizational doctrine remain separate.

### Tactical Effectiveness vs Doctrine

Example:

```text
Explosive breach:
tactically effective

Context:
civilian Haven
high collateral risk
shared survival ethic
```

The game may establish:

```text
TACTICALLY_EFFECTIVE
DOCTRINALLY_RESTRICTED
```

without contradiction.

### DISENGAGEMENT_DECISION_III

Supports determining when continued fighting no longer serves the objective.

Possible reasons include:

```text
objective complete
enemy force too strong
casualty risk too high
withdrawal window closing
reinforcement imminent
objective lost
alternate approach preferable
hostage risk unacceptable
```

SO2 executes a fighting withdrawal.

SO3 determines whether disengagement is the correct tactical decision.

### Disengagement Is Not Failure

Potential outcomes include:

```text
TACTICAL_DISENGAGEMENT
ORDERLY_WITHDRAWAL
OBJECTIVE_COMPLETE_WITHDRAW
OBJECTIVE_ABORTED_WITHDRAW
FORCE_PRESERVATION_WITHDRAWAL
```

Mission evaluation remains separate.

### FORCE_ADAPTATION_III

Supports changing how existing combat capability is being used.

Examples:

```text
switch from advance to delay
switch from route control to extraction
change target priority
move reserve to threatened route

shift from capture attempt to withdrawal
change from direct attack to containment
```

Technician adapts equipment and systems.

SO3 adapts force employment.

### Tactical Outcome vs Mission Outcome

Critical distinction:

```text

≠

```

Example:

```text

```

Mission may still succeed.

Or:

```text

all hostiles defeated

Mission:
artifact destroyed
```

Mission may fail.

## Cross-Profession Boundaries

### Scout Boundary

Scout commonly establishes:

```text
where movement occurred
which routes connect
where patrols travel
where groups are likely going
where access appears hidden
```

Soldier commonly establishes:

```text
which route matters during combat
which threat controls movement
where pressure should be applied
which objective must be defended
when to withdraw or escalate
```

Useful handoff:

```text
Scout:
"They pass this junction every six minutes."

Soldier:
"Then this junction controls the engagement if contact starts here."
```

### Technician Boundary

Soldier:

```text
"The turret stopped tracking us."
```

Technician:

```text
"The tracking actuator failed."
```

Soldier understands combat behavior.

Technician understands technical mechanism.

### Scientist Boundary

Soldier:

```text
"The creature becomes vulnerable immediately after attacking."
```

Scientist:

```text
"The attack causes a temporary metabolic collapse."
```

Soldier may exploit a known pattern without understanding why it exists.

### Diplomat Boundary

Soldier may recognize:

```text
surrender
disengagement
loss of immediate threat
```

Diplomat handles:

```text
terms
negotiation
intent through communication
authority
political consequences
social leverage
```

Recognizing surrender is not the same as negotiating what happens next.

## Specialization Boundary

The base Soldier progression must preserve the existing specialization space.

### Marksman

Owns exceptional offensive exploitation such as:

```text
opening damage
precision attack
superior use of offensive windows
```

Base Soldier may recognize an opening.

Marksman turns it into an exceptional offensive result.

### Tactician

Owns exceptional:

```text
initiative
coordination
tempo
command advantage
```

Base SO3 may organize and direct ordinary tactical activity.

Tactician makes that coordination mechanically exceptional.

### Guardian

Owns exceptional:

```text
interception
ally protection
damage prevention
```

Base Soldier may take ordinary protective actions.

Guardian makes protection a defining combat capability.

## Final Profession Standard

A Soldier should never function as a generic combat-stat bonus.

The Profession should create gameplay by answering progressively broader tactical questions:

```text
SO1:
What is threatening us right now,
and how do I fight it safely?

SO2:
What controls this engagement,
and how can we reshape it?

SO3:
What actually decides whether the mission succeeds,
and how should our available force be used?
```

The Soldier's power is therefore not:

```text
more damage
```

It is:

```text
better threat judgment
+
better force application
+
better engagement control
+
better objective-centered tactics
+
better decisions about escalation and withdrawal
```
