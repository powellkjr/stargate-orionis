## Profession Identity

**Code:** `TE`

Technician progression is built around three distinct capability tiers:
 `TE1 — Diagnose & Restore TE2 — Adapt TE3 — Implement `
The progression is intentionally qualitative, not just “same actions at higher numbers.”


- **TE1** understands ordinary equipment well enough to operate, diagnose, service, repair, stabilize, and restore it.

- **TE2** understands systems well enough to modify, fabricate, integrate, reroute, calibrate, and adapt them while staying within a defensible operating envelope.

- **TE3** understands implementation deeply enough to translate known requirements and principles into a working physical solution.


Technician competency never substitutes for missing subject knowledge.

A Technician may understand **how to engineer**, but still needs the relevant Theory to understand **what they are engineering**.

## Core Profession Boundary

Technician Curriculum provides implementation method. It does not substitute for missing subject Theory, establish unknown scientific truth, or automatically grant specialized Tool or Room Services.

## Qualification Progression

Technician progression follows `TE0 → TE1 → TE2 → TE3 → Specialization or Cross-Path`. Certification demonstrates competency at each tier; it does not simulate the full education period.

## Tier I — TE1 — Diagnose & Restore

### Core Promise


Understand ordinary equipment, find ordinary faults, restore intended operation, and stabilize ordinary technical failures.


### Qualification Transition
 `TE0 → TE1 `
### Nominal Certification Budget
 `8 hours `
### Certification Structure
 `1. Written Examination 2. Commission Technician Tools I 3. Technical Fundamentals 4. System Diagnosis 5. Technical Service 6. Systems Access 7. Field Response 8. Recovery & Verification  COMPLETE → TE1 `
The final state transition occurs when the Recipe completes. It does not require a separate ninth bubble.

### TE1 Competencies
 `TECHNICAL_OPERATION_I TECHNICAL_SAFETY_I TECHNICAL_INSPECTION_I TECHNICAL_DIAGNOSTICS_I  ELECTRICAL_SERVICE_I MECHANICAL_SERVICE_I FLUID_SYSTEMS_I SYSTEMS_ACCESS_I  TECHNICAL_REPAIR_I FIELD_EXPEDIENT_REPAIR_I TECHNICAL_RECOVERY_I OPERATIONAL_VERIFICATION_I `
### TECHNICAL_OPERATION_I

Supports:


- operating ordinary understood equipment;

- recognizing controls and indicators;

- normal startup/shutdown;

- following documented operating procedure;

- recognizing documented operating limits.


### TECHNICAL_SAFETY_I

Supports:


- recognizing common technical hazards;

- energy isolation;

- safe shutdown;

- making ordinary technical equipment safe before work;

- identifying unsafe operating conditions.


Associated Resolution Types may include:
 `TECHNICAL_SHUTDOWN_I POWER_ISOLATION_I `
### TECHNICAL_INSPECTION_I

Supports identifying:


- components;

- visible damage;

- wear;

- tampering;

- incorrect installation;

- obvious abnormal state;

- ordinary maintenance problems.


### TECHNICAL_DIAGNOSTICS_I

TE1 diagnostics answer:


Where is the ordinary fault?


Supports:


- standard measurements;

- documented diagnostic procedure;

- component testing;

- connection testing;

- tracing straightforward faults;

- identifying an immediate ordinary failure cause.


Associated Resolution Type:
 `TECHNICAL_ASSESSMENT_I `
### ELECTRICAL_SERVICE_I

Supports ordinary:


- wiring;

- electrical connections;

- component replacement;

- circuit servicing;

- electrical fault correction.


Associated Resolution Type:
 `ELECTRICAL_STABILIZATION_I `
### MECHANICAL_SERVICE_I

Supports ordinary:


- disassembly;

- adjustment;

- maintenance;

- mechanical repair;

- servicing assemblies;

- component replacement.


Associated Resolution Type:
 `MECHANICAL_STABILIZATION_I `
### FLUID_SYSTEMS_I

Supports ordinary:


- valves;

- pumps;

- pressure systems;

- coolant systems;

- hydraulic/pneumatic systems;

- flow inspection;

- leak location;

- section isolation;

- basic servicing of lines, couplings, and pumps.


Associated Resolution Type:
 `LEAK_ISOLATION_I `
### SYSTEMS_ACCESS_I

Supports work on understood ordinary control systems:


- identify technical interfaces;

- connect through standard service interfaces;

- navigate maintenance/service controls;

- recognize basic access restrictions;

- use documented maintenance/admin paths;

- bypass basic technical lockouts;

- restore normal configuration.


Associated Resolution Type:
 `CONTROL_RECOVERY_I `
This does **not** inherently grant:


- universal hacking;

- cryptanalysis;

- military intrusion expertise;

- unknown software exploitation;

- advanced cybersecurity knowledge.


Those require relevant subject Theory.

### TECHNICAL_REPAIR_I

Supports ordinary repair methodology.

It does **not** mean:


TE1 can repair anything.


Actual repair still depends on:
 `Profession competency + subject Theory + Tool Service + Room Service/context `
### FIELD_EXPEDIENT_REPAIR_I

Supports:


- stabilizing ordinary equipment;

- temporary repair;

- field patching;

- bypassing a failed nonessential element where appropriate;

- restoring safe degraded operation;

- making an ordinary system usable until permanent repair.


Associated Resolution Types:
 `TECHNICAL_BYPASS_I TECHNICAL_CONTAINMENT_I EMERGENCY_REPAIR_I `
### TECHNICAL_RECOVERY_I

Supports:


- safely disconnecting useful components;

- removing components without unnecessary destruction;

- ordinary technical salvage/recovery;

- preserving useful recovered equipment.


### OPERATIONAL_VERIFICATION_I

Supports:


- reassembly;

- restoring valid configuration;

- testing after work;

- determining whether equipment functions normally;

- deciding whether it is safe to return to service.

### TE1 Capability Boundary

The shorthand is:
 `TE1 — REPAIR / REPLACE "Use the right part." `
TE1 does not inherently fabricate replacement components.

A TE1 may:


- patch;

- fit ordinary consumables;

- use compatible replacements;

- perform ordinary field repairs.


But genuine component fabrication belongs at TE2.

## Tier II — TE2 — Adapt

### Core Promise


Understand an existing technical system well enough to modify, fabricate, integrate, reroute, calibrate, and adapt it while maintaining a defensible operating envelope.


### Qualification Transition
 `TE1 → TE2 `
### Nominal Certification Budget
 `12 hours `
### Certification Structure
 `1.  Advanced Technical Examination 2.  Commission Technician Tools II 3.  Characterize Technical System 4.  Diagnose Complex Fault 5.  Fabricate Technical Component 6.  Disassemble & Reconstruct System 7.  Modify Technical System 8.  Integrate Technical Systems 9.  Evaluate Load & Compatibility 10. Reconfigure & Bypass Controls 11. Align & Calibrate System 12. Implement Expedient Adaptation  COMPLETE → TE2 `
### TE2 Competencies
 `TECHNICAL_CHARACTERIZATION_II ADVANCED_DIAGNOSTICS_II  FABRICATION_II TECHNICAL_DISASSEMBLY_II  SYSTEM_MODIFICATION_II SYSTEM_INTEGRATION_II LOAD_COMPATIBILITY_II CONTROL_RECONFIGURATION_II  CONTROLLED_BYPASS_II ALIGNMENT_CALIBRATION_II EXPEDIENT_ADAPTATION_II `
### TECHNICAL_CHARACTERIZATION_II

Supports determining how an understood system behaves, even if it is unfamiliar.

Can include:


- trace subsystem relationships;

- identify inputs/outputs;

- identify operating ranges;

- measure loads;

- characterize performance;

- determine interface behavior;

- compare observed behavior to documentation.


Characterization does not require the system to be broken.

### ADVANCED_DIAGNOSTICS_II

TE2 diagnostics answer:


Why is the understood system behaving incorrectly?


Supports:


- interacting subsystem faults;

- intermittent faults;

- incomplete documentation;

- reproducing failure conditions;

- diagnosing load-dependent failure;

- distinguishing symptoms from contributing failures;

- diagnosing problems caused by modification/integration.


Possible Resolution support:
 `ADVANCED_TECHNICAL_ASSESSMENT_II `
### FABRICATION_II

TE2 can fabricate from an understood definition.

Supports:


- interpret technical drawings;

- interpret specifications;

- measure existing components;

- produce ordinary fabrication specifications;

- select appropriate materials/processes;

- fabricate replacement/adaptation components;

- maintain meaningful tolerances;

- inspect finished components against specification.


TE2 may also derive a component specification from an intact understood example.

### TECHNICAL_DISASSEMBLY_II

Supports:


- determining safe disassembly sequence;

- preserving interfaces;

- preserving useful components;

- protecting tolerances;

- controlled teardown;

- reconstruction of known systems after disassembly.


### SYSTEM_MODIFICATION_II

Supports:


- deliberate alteration of understood systems;

- controlled system changes;

- evaluating consequences of modification;

- preserving required function;

- maintaining safe operation.


Possible Resolution support:
 `SYSTEM_COMPENSATION_II `
### SYSTEM_INTEGRATION_II

Supports taking understood systems that were not designed to work together and making them compatible.

Can include:


- interface characterization;

- adapters;

- ordinary signal conversion;

- power adaptation;

- fluid/mechanical interface adaptation;

- control integration;

- reconciling operating ranges;

- validating combined operation.


Possible Resolution support:
 `TEMPORARY_INTEGRATION_II `
### LOAD_COMPATIBILITY_II

Supports:


- comparing electrical loads;

- mechanical loads;

- fluid requirements;

- interface ranges;

- operating ranges;

- expected demand;

- conversion requirements;

- safe limits;

- determining whether an integration is sustainable.


Possible Resolution support:
 `LOAD_SHEDDING_II `
Example field result:


This generator can power the shield, but not while the primary pump is running.


### CONTROL_RECONFIGURATION_II

Supports:


- tracing control relationships;

- modifying known configuration;

- changing control routing;

- altering thresholds/interlocks within understood limits;

- establishing temporary/manual control paths;

- integrating mismatched control interfaces;

- validating resulting restrictions.


Possible Resolution support:
 `CONTROL_RECONFIGURATION_II `
### CONTROLLED_BYPASS_II

Supports:


- bypassing failed subsystems;

- rerouting around understood failures;

- maintaining controlled degraded operation;

- establishing safe temporary operating conditions.


Possible Resolution support:
 `SYSTEM_REROUTE_II `
### ALIGNMENT_CALIBRATION_II

Supports:


- alignment beyond routine servicing;

- equipment calibration;

- system tuning;

- checking performance against specification;

- correcting known alignment/calibration problems.


### EXPEDIENT_ADAPTATION_II

Supports:


- nonstandard but technically defensible solutions;

- substitute components;

- temporary interfaces;

- rerouting;

- improvised adaptation;

- making available resources satisfy known requirements;

- establishing a new safe operating envelope after modification.


Possible Resolution support:
 `COMPONENT_SUBSTITUTION_II `
The key rule is:


TE2 may depart from the original implementation, but the resulting configuration remains within a defensible safe operating envelope.

### TE2 Capability Boundary

Shorthand:
 `TE2 — ADAPT / FABRICATE "Make or modify the part." `
A TE2 can:


- fabricate from definition;

- adapt existing components;

- integrate systems;

- modify configuration;

- reroute;

- substitute;

- recalibrate.


They are not yet expected to derive a wholly new implementation from high-level technical requirements.

## Tier III — TE3 — Implement

### Core Promise


Given known principles, requirements, constraints, and available infrastructure, determine and realize a working physical implementation.


### Qualification Transition
 `TE2 → TE3 `
### Nominal Certification Budget

Currently:
 `12 hours `
This remains provisional only in the sense that Profession III duration has not been globally benchmarked beyond Technician.

### Certification Structure
 `1.  Senior Technical Examination 2.  Commission Technician Tools III 3.  Develop Implementation Design 4.  Develop Implementation Plan 5.  Fabricate Precision Assembly 6.  Design Complex Integration 7.  Diagnose System-Level Failure 8.  Implement Control System 9.  Reconstruct Incomplete Implementation 10. Implement Technical Substitution 11. Commission & Characterize System 12. Validate Complete Implementation  COMPLETE → TE3 `
### TE3 Competencies
 `IMPLEMENTATION_DESIGN_III IMPLEMENTATION_PLANNING_III  PRECISION_FABRICATION_III INTEGRATION_DESIGN_III  SYSTEM_LEVEL_DIAGNOSTICS_III CONTROL_IMPLEMENTATION_III  TECHNICAL_RECONSTRUCTION_III IMPLEMENTATION_SUBSTITUTION_III  COMMISSIONING_III SYSTEM_VALIDATION_III `
### IMPLEMENTATION_DESIGN_III

Given:


- defined capability requirements;

- known technical principles;

- known constraints;


TE3 can:


- decompose implementation into buildable assemblies;

- select appropriate known technical approaches;

- establish physical interfaces;

- determine component relationships;

- reconcile implementation constraints;

- determine what must be fabricated, integrated, or modified.


It does not inherently allow the Technician to establish unknown scientific principles.

### IMPLEMENTATION_PLANNING_III

Supports:


- turning an implementation design into an executable build sequence;

- determining fabrication order;

- determining assembly order;

- identifying required Tool Services;

- identifying required Room Services;

- establishing tolerances;

- sequencing installation and commissioning.


### PRECISION_FABRICATION_III

Supports:


- complex one-off fabrication;

- establishing tolerances where documentation is incomplete;

- precision components;

- complex assemblies;

- implementation-specific fabrication planning.


This does not mean TET3 automatically provides every specialized manufacturing capability.

### INTEGRATION_DESIGN_III

Supports:


- designing how multiple understood technologies interact;

- defining subsystem boundaries;

- creating new interfaces;

- determining conversion requirements;

- coordinating interacting controls;

- resolving cascading dependencies;

- defining startup/shutdown sequencing;

- validating system-level interaction.


This is different from TE2 integration.

TE2 asks:


How do I make these existing systems work together?


TE3 asks:


How should these systems interact as part of the implementation I am building?


### SYSTEM_LEVEL_DIAGNOSTICS_III

TE3 diagnostics answer:


What interaction or implementation is actually causing the system-level failure?


Supports:


- multi-cause faults;

- cascading failures;

- cross-subsystem diagnostics;

- root-cause analysis;

- feedback problems;

- distinguishing component failure from integration/configuration/design problems;

- determining corrective implementation requirements.


Example:


The pump failure is secondary. Regulator oscillation is destabilizing the cooling controller and causing cavitation.


### CONTROL_IMPLEMENTATION_III

Supports:


- designing a replacement control path;

- defining sensing/actuation relationships;

- implementing known control logic;

- replacing missing controller behavior;

- integrating multiple control systems;

- implementing fallback/failsafe behavior;

- commissioning new control implementations.


It does not inherently grant knowledge of unknown software, encryption, or foreign security systems.

### TECHNICAL_RECONSTRUCTION_III

Hard rule:


**A Technician III may reconstruct missing implementation, but may not reconstruct missing knowledge.**


Supports:


- reconstructing missing physical implementation from known requirements;

- inferring implementation relationships from surviving evidence;

- replacing missing implementation with an equivalent known solution;

- recreating missing interfaces;

- deriving dimensions/tolerances from known constraints;

- documenting engineering assumptions.


Does **not** inherently:


- reveal hidden tags;

- substitute for Analysis;

- create Theory;

- infer unknown scientific principles;

- determine unknown function simply because the Technician is highly skilled;

- bypass missing subject Theory.


Useful information-state distinction:
 `Blueprint known → TE1/TE2 may be enough depending on complexity.  Blueprint incomplete, principles known → TE3 can reconstruct implementation.  Principles unknown → Analysis / Research / subject Theory required. `
### IMPLEMENTATION_SUBSTITUTION_III

Supports:


- redesigning around unavailable components;

- selecting functionally equivalent approaches;

- changing implementation architecture while preserving required capability;

- evaluating consequences of substitution;

- engineering around infrastructure/material constraints.


Difference from reconstruction:
 `Substitution: "I know what belongs here, but I don't have it."  Reconstruction: "The original implementation is incomplete, but I know what it must accomplish." `
### COMMISSIONING_III

Supports:


- bringing unfamiliar/new machinery into operation;

- controlled startup;

- observing system behavior during startup;

- identifying implementation defects;

- establishing operating boundaries;

- determining whether the implementation is ready for service.


This is system commissioning, distinct from Tool commissioning.

### SYSTEM_VALIDATION_III

Supports:


- validating implementation against requirements;

- testing under load;

- characterizing performance;

- identifying limitations;

- determining failure boundaries;

- confirming the completed implementation satisfies constraints.

### TE3 Capability Boundary

Shorthand:
 `TE3 — DESIGN / IMPLEMENT "Determine what the part or system needs to be." `
A TE3 can turn:
 `known principles + known requirements + known constraints + available infrastructure `
into:
 `implementation design → build plan → component definitions → integration → commissioning → validation `
But TE3 cannot invent missing subject knowledge.

## Tools and Room Services

Tool commissioning is part of certification.

Generic/general tools become profession-specific tools through an actual Recipe bubble.
 `Generic Tool → Technician Tools I  Technician Tools I → Technician Tools II  Technician Tools II → Technician Tools III `
Tool commissioning happens early enough in certification that the candidate can demonstrate later competencies using their newly commissioned tools.

Tool capability and Profession competency are separate.

A highly trained Technician without the required Tool Service may understand what must be done but still be unable to execute it.

Likewise, advanced tools do not grant missing Profession competency.

### Tool Services

#### TET1 — Service

Supports ordinary:


- diagnosis;

- maintenance;

- disassembly;

- replacement;

- repair;

- electrical measurement;

- mechanical measurement;

- isolation;

- wiring;

- soldering;

- component replacement;

- field patching.


#### TET2 — Modify

Includes lower-tier capability and supports:


- precision measurement;

- cutting;

- shaping;

- drilling;

- ordinary machining;

- joining;

- fabrication;

- controlled disassembly;

- interface fabrication;

- advanced diagnostics;

- signal tracing;

- load testing;

- bypass;

- modification;

- alignment;

- calibration.


#### TET3 — Engineer

Includes lower-tier capability and supports:


- high-precision measurement;

- complex system diagnostics;

- multi-channel instrumentation;

- advanced signal tracing;

- precision fabrication setup;

- complex alignment;

- commissioning;

- characterization under load;

- one-off assemblies;

TET3 does not automatically expose every specialized fabrication capability.

Separate Tool Services may still exist, such as:
 `PS CNC ASGARD_FAB INSPECTION `
A physical Tool may expose any valid combination of Tool Services.

Recipes require Tool Service tags, not physical Tool identities.

## Scientist Handoff

This section records the locked conceptual boundary between Technician implementation and Scientist explanation.

### Technician and Scientist Boundary

This distinction is locked conceptually.

```text
SCIENTIST
What is true?
Why does it happen?
What conditions govern it?
What must be true for the desired phenomenon to exist?
```

```text
TECHNICIAN
How do we physically realize those known conditions?
How do we build, repair, adapt, integrate, or implement the system?
```

Example:

```text
SC3:
Stable containment requires this geometry,
energy density, phase relationship, and thermal ceiling.

TE3:
Given those requirements, I can design and implement
hardware that produces them.
```

Technician may perform sophisticated measurements.

Scientist may operate sophisticated equipment.

The Profession distinction is based on reasoning method and purpose, not possession of instruments.

## Progression Summary

### Fabrication Progression

`TE1 — REPAIR / REPLACE Use the right part.  TE2 — ADAPT / FABRICATE Make or modify the part.  TE3 — DESIGN / IMPLEMENT Determine what the part needs to be. `
Example:

**TE1**


The bearing failed. Give me the correct replacement.


**TE2**


We don't have the bearing. I can fabricate a suitable replacement.


**TE3**


We don't need to reproduce the original bearing arrangement. I can redesign that assembly to satisfy the known requirements using what we have.

### Diagnostics Progression

`TE1 — LOCATE Where is the ordinary fault?  TE2 — CHARACTERIZE Why is the understood system behaving incorrectly?  TE3 — ROOT CAUSE What interaction or implementation is actually causing the system-level failure? `

### Systems Access / Control Progression

`TE1 — SYSTEMS_ACCESS_I Access and bypass ordinary known controls.  TE2 — CONTROL_RECONFIGURATION_II Change how an understood control system is configured or routed.  TE3 — CONTROL_IMPLEMENTATION_III Create a new control implementation from known requirements. `
Technician is not synonymous with generic “hacker.”

Cybersecurity, cryptography, intrusion, foreign protocols, and unknown software remain subject-Theory concerns.

### Integration Progression

`TE1 — CONNECT Use standard compatible interfaces.  TE2 — SYSTEM_INTEGRATION_II Adapt understood systems so they can work together.  TE3 — INTEGRATION_DESIGN_III Design how multiple understood systems interact inside a new implementation. `
Future specialization boundary:
 `Integrations — EXPLOIT Create useful unintended interactions between systems. `
Base Technician makes systems work together correctly.

Integrations specialization explores useful relationships beyond normal design intent.

### Improvisation / Overdrive Boundary

`TE1 — FIELD_EXPEDIENT_REPAIR_I Restore or stabilize intended function using available means.  TE2 — EXPEDIENT_ADAPTATION_II Create a nonstandard but technically defensible solution.  TE3 — IMPLEMENTATION_SUBSTITUTION_III Redesign around constraints while validating the resulting operating envelope.  Overdrive Specialization Intentionally exceed or abuse the validated operating envelope in exchange for immediate capability. `
This is the core line between base Technician and Overdrive.

A normal Technician may work in dangerous conditions.

But base Technician competency does not inherently rely on knowingly unsafe technical operation.

Example:

**TE2**


I can reroute cooling and cap output at 65%.


**TE3**


I can redesign the cooling implementation around what we have.


**Overdrive**


I can disable the thermal protection and give you 120% output for as long as the hardware survives.

### Technician vs Scientist Boundary

Technician III does not decide unknown scientific truth.

The intended split is:

**Scientist**


Determines principles, models, requirements, and what must be true for the solution to work.


**Technician III**


Determines how those known principles and requirements can be physically implemented with the available technology and infrastructure.


So:
 `Scientist: "What field geometry is required?"  Technician III: "Given that geometry, I can build an emitter arrangement that produces it." `
This boundary is especially important for alien technology.

## Reference Summary

`TE1 — DIAGNOSE & RESTORE Operate. Inspect. Diagnose. Service. Repair. Stabilize. Recover. Verify.  TE2 — ADAPT Characterize. Diagnose interacting faults. Fabricate. Disassemble intelligently. Modify. Integrate. Evaluate compatibility. Reconfigure. Bypass. Calibrate. Adapt.  TE3 — IMPLEMENT Design implementation. Plan execution. Precision fabricate. Design integration. Diagnose root causes. Implement controls. Reconstruct missing implementation. Substitute unavailable implementation. Commission. Validate. `
The central Technician progression is:


**Something exists → make it work.** **Something exists → change it to meet the need.** **A capability needs to exist → determine how to build it.**

## Final Profession Standard

The Profession Curriculum provides trained method and does not substitute for missing subject Theory, Tool Services, Room Services, or valid current state.
