## Profession Identity

**Code:** `DI`

Diplomat progression is built around increasingly broad social and political judgment, from handling an immediate interaction, to constructing agreements across competing interests, to representing the SGC across larger political relationships.

The core progression is:

```text
DI1 — Communicate & Influence
"What does this person want, and what can change their immediate decision?"

DI2 — Negotiate & Resolve
"What conflict, interests, or obligations are keeping these parties apart, and what agreement could resolve it?"

DI3 — Align & Represent
"What larger political relationship is at stake, and how should the SGC position itself?"
```

The Profession-wide principle is:

> Diplomat Curriculum supplies communication method, social interpretation, negotiation method, commitment design, and institutional representation. Culture, Faction Practice, agreement state, authority, and other subject Theory supply what the social situation actually means.

A high-tier Diplomat does not automatically understand unknown cultures, hidden motives, faction policy, legal authority, or another Actor's internal thoughts.

Diplomat changes choices through:

```text
communication
credibility
legitimacy
interest
leverage
agreement
trust
obligation
framing
```

Diplomat does not directly control another Actor's mind.

## Core Profession Boundary

Diplomat answers:

```text
What does this person appear to want?
What authority do they have?
What do they care about?
What would make voluntary cooperation more likely?
What interests or obligations prevent agreement?
What terms could reconcile the parties?
What commitments already constrain the SGC?
What larger political relationship is at stake?
What must remain true for that relationship to function?
```

Soldier answers:

```text
What is the immediate combat threat?
When has surrender or disengagement occurred?
How should force be applied?
```

Scout answers:

```text
Where did people move?
What routes or locations matter?
What field Evidence exists?
```

Technician answers:

```text
How does the physical system work?
What technical mechanism creates the current condition?
```

Medic answers:

```text
What is happening to the patient?
What treatment is appropriate?
```

Scientist answers:

```text
What phenomenon is occurring?
What general explanation best fits the Evidence?
```

The critical Diplomat principle is:

> Diplomat interprets and changes voluntary social relationships without replacing physical Reality, hidden intent, authority, faction doctrine, or the decisions of other Actors.

## Influence and Profession Competency

Influence remains a universal stat.

Diplomat competency determines what trained social methods are available.

```text
Influence stat
=
how effectively a Unit performs a social interaction

Diplomat competency
=
what social methods the Unit knows,
what social questions they can answer,
and what kinds of agreements or political structures they can construct
```

A high-Influence non-Diplomat may still be personally convincing.

A Diplomat has systematic competency in:

```text
communication
social observation
intent assessment
authority recognition
interest analysis
persuasion
framing
negotiation
mediation
commitment design
political representation
```

Influence is not permission.

Competency is not guaranteed success.

## Tier I — DI1 — Communicate & Influence

### Core Promise

> Understand immediate social situations, communicate clearly across known cultural contexts, recognize intent and authority, and use legitimate influence to change voluntary behavior.

Core question:

> **What does this person want, what do they believe is happening, and what can I say or offer that may change their decision?**

Shorthand:

```text
COMMUNICATE / INFLUENCE
```

DI1's defining principle is:

> Understand the immediate social situation, identify who matters and what they care about, and create voluntary cooperation where a defensible social path exists.

### DI1 Certification

```text
1. Diplomatic Foundations Examination
2. Commission Diplomat Tools I
3. Establish Communication
4. Assess Intent & Disposition
5. Identify Authority & Interest
6. Apply Persuasion & Framing
7. Manage Immediate Social Conflict
8. Confirm Terms & Handoff

COMPLETE → DI1
```

### DIPLOMATIC_SAFETY_I

Supports recognizing ordinary known social risks associated with communication and negotiation.

Examples:

```text
known taboo
insult risk
status violation
improper form of address
sensitive topic
known ritual expectation
obvious coercion
negotiation under duress
```

Subject Theory remains mandatory.

For a known culture:

```text
DIPLOMATIC_SAFETY_I
+
relevant Culture or Faction Practice Theory
=
ordinary social-hazard recognition
```

For an unknown culture:

```text
gesture meaning unknown
status symbols unknown
social convention unknown
```

may be the correct result.

Diplomat does not automatically understand every culture.

### COMMUNICATION_I

Supports establishing reliable ordinary communication where a valid language, translation method, or known protocol exists.

Possible tasks include:

```text
introduce self
clarify role
state immediate purpose
ask direct questions
confirm understanding
correct misunderstanding
establish communication channel
```

Critical distinctions:

```text
UNDERSTANDING
≠
AGREEMENT
```

and:

```text
TRANSLATION
≠
CULTURAL_INTERPRETATION
```

A device may translate words perfectly while missing their social or political meaning.

### SOCIAL_OBSERVATION_I

Supports recording socially relevant observable behavior.

Examples:

```text
speaker repeatedly looks toward armed guard
leader allows another person to answer
group becomes quiet when faction name is mentioned
individual avoids discussing missing equipment
crowd reacts positively to local leader
speaker interrupts subordinate
```

These are observations.

They are not automatically:

```text
FEAR
GUILT
DECEPTION
LOYALTY
```

Those require interpretation and sufficient Evidence.

### INTENT_ASSESSMENT_I

Supports bounded interpretation of immediate expressed or observable intent.

Potential findings include:

```text
WILLING_TO_TALK
REFUSING_REQUEST
SEEKING_ASSISTANCE
SEEKING_PAYMENT
ATTEMPTING_TO_LEAVE
TRYING_TO_DELAY
ISSUING_WARNING
REQUESTING_AUTHORITY
INTENT_UNCLEAR
```

This is not mind reading.

The valid relationship is:

```text
OBSERVED_BEHAVIOR
+
COMMUNICATION
+
KNOWN_CONTEXT
→
bounded intent interpretation
```

not:

```text
Diplomat
→
hidden true motive revealed
```

### DISPOSITION_ASSESSMENT_I

Supports assessing immediate attitude toward:

```text
the SGC
the current request
the specific Diplomat
another party
the current situation
```

Potential contextual findings may include:

```text
COOPERATIVE
RECEPTIVE
NEUTRAL
RELUCTANT
SUSPICIOUS
HOSTILE
AFRAID
```

Disposition should be dynamic and contextual.

It is not the same as long-term relationship.

Example:

```text
Haven leader:
generally friendly toward SGC

Current disposition:
HOSTILE toward request to inspect military warehouse
```

### AUTHORITY_RECOGNITION_I

Supports determining who appears to hold legitimate or practical authority for the current matter.

Possible findings include:

```text
formal leader
authorized representative
technical authority
military authority
property owner
custodian
witness only
unauthorized intermediary
authority unclear
```

Not every problem belongs to the highest-ranking NPC.

Example:

```text
Governor:
political authority

Princess:
biological or cultural authority where Moy'na governance establishes it

Warehouse supervisor:
local operational authority
```

### INTEREST_IDENTIFICATION_I

Supports identifying what a party appears to care about in the immediate interaction.

Examples:

```text
safety
payment
recognition
access
privacy
time
status
medical aid
protection
return of property
information
avoidance of conflict
```

Important distinction:

```text
POSITION
≠
INTEREST
```

Example:

```text
Position:
"No SGC personnel enter the facility."

Possible interest:
"Do not expose the illegal reactor modification."
```

DI1 may identify an obvious interest where Evidence supports it.

Complex or hidden interests belong later or require more Evidence.

### PERSUASION_I

Supports ordinary attempts to change another party's voluntary decision using a defensible argument.

Possible methods include:

```text
appeal to shared interest
explain consequence
offer reassurance
provide relevant Evidence
make ordinary request
invoke known mutual benefit
appeal to established relationship
```

Conceptually:

```text
valid social method
+
known interest
+
credible argument
+
Influence performance
+
current disposition
=
persuasion attempt
```

Persuasion never guarantees agreement.

### Influence Is Not Mind Control

This is a hard architecture rule.

```text
SUCCESSFUL_PERSUASION
```

means:

> The target voluntarily accepted the argument or request under the current circumstances.

It does not mean the target's preferences were overwritten.

A target may still refuse because:

```text
cost too high
belief incompatible
authority lacking
risk unacceptable
promise not credible
other obligation stronger
cultural rule forbids
```

No amount of generic Influence should automatically override a hard semantic conflict.

### FRAMING_I

Supports presenting the same legitimate request in a way relevant to the listener's known interests.

Example request:

```text
Allow SGC inspection of damaged power facility.
```

To technician:

```text
"We need to determine whether the damage can spread to your distribution network."
```

To governor:

```text
"We need to determine whether the failure threatens the Haven."
```

To security chief:

```text
"We need to confirm whether this was sabotage."
```

The request remains the same.

The framing changes.

### Framing Cannot Fabricate Reality

DI1 cannot claim:

```text
"The reactor will explode in ten minutes."
```

unless the SGC has supporting Evidence.

False claims belong to deception, not framing.

### DEESCALATION_I

Supports ordinary communication intended to reduce immediate hostility or prevent avoidable violence.

Possible methods include:

```text
clarify misunderstanding
acknowledge concern
state non-hostile intent
offer temporary pause
reduce visible threat
request separation
establish speaking representative
confirm boundaries
```

Potential outcomes include:

```text
WILLING_TO_TALK
HOSTILITY_REDUCED
TEMPORARY_PAUSE
VIOLENCE_DELAYED
TERMS_REQUESTED
```

where valid.

### BASIC_NEGOTIATION_I

Supports simple bilateral exchanges where the main interests and authority are already understood.

Examples:

```text
information for payment
temporary access for supervision
release of property after verification
medical aid in exchange for safe passage
short-term ceasefire
ordinary trade term
```

DI1 does not yet own complex multi-party diplomacy or faction-wide agreements.

### Negotiation Needs Real Terms

Avoid making the primary diplomatic result:

```text
NEGOTIATION_SUCCESS
+ reputation
```

Prefer actual terms:

```text
SGC receives access to facility
Haven receives replacement power cells
access valid for six hours
heavy weapons prohibited
Haven engineer accompanies team
```

Diplomacy becomes systemic when it changes real permissions, obligations, and restrictions.

### AGREEMENT_CONFIRMATION_I

Supports confirming that parties share the same understanding of negotiated terms.

Potential findings include:

```text
TERMS_CONFIRMED
TERMS_AMBIGUOUS
AUTHORITY_NOT_CONFIRMED
OBLIGATION_UNCLEAR
AGREEMENT_NOT_REACHED
```

This helps avoid future disputes caused by mismatched understanding.

### DIPLOMATIC_DOCUMENTATION_I

Supports recording:

```text
statements
requests
offers
terms
authority claims
witnesses
agreements
refusals
warnings
commitments
unresolved questions
```

These become institutional Knowledge where recording is available.

Provenance must be preserved.

Example:

```text
Governor stated:
"No CLP personnel entered the warehouse."
```

is not equivalent to:

```text
No CLP personnel entered the warehouse.
```

The first is testimony.

The second requires additional Evidence.

### DIPLOMATIC_HANDOFF_I

Supports transferring socially relevant Knowledge.

Examples:

```text
threatening statement → Soldier
technical claim → Technician
medical claim → Medic
scientific claim → Scientist
movement testimony → Scout
faction commitment → mission / diplomacy systems
```

Example:

```text
Witness:
"I heard machinery running under the old water plant last night."
```

Diplomat records testimony.

Scout may investigate the location.

Technician may later inspect the machinery.

The testimony does not become physical truth merely because it was recorded correctly.

### DI1 Knowledge Authority

DI1 normally originates:

```text
OBSERVATION
EVIDENCE
INTERPRETATION
QUESTION
INSTANCE_FINDING
```

Common social findings may include:

```text
STATED_INTENT
INTENT_FINDING
DISPOSITION_FINDING
AUTHORITY_FINDING
INTEREST_FINDING
AGREEMENT_FINDING
SOCIAL_RISK_FINDING
```

DI1 should not normally originate generalized Culture or Faction Practice Theory.

### Authority Invocation

DI1 may invoke real existing authority.

Example:

```text
"We are acting under the governor's signed authorization."
```

This may create a compliance opportunity where jurisdiction is recognized.

It is not the same as ordinary persuasion.

### Authority Is Scoped

Authority should rarely be a universal boolean.

It needs scope such as:

```text
source
subject
jurisdiction
duration
```

Example:

```text
Governor authorization:
inspect municipal power systems

Does not imply:
inspect private residence
```

### DI1 Capability Boundary

DI1 can:

* establish communication where language or protocol capability exists;
* recognize ordinary social hazards;
* record social observations;
* assess bounded immediate intent;
* assess current disposition;
* identify relevant authority;
* identify obvious interests;
* make ordinary persuasive arguments;
* frame requests;
* deescalate immediate conflict;
* negotiate simple bilateral terms;
* perform plausible basic Bluff;
* invoke legitimate authority;
* confirm agreements;
* document statements and commitments.

DI1 does not inherently:

* read minds;
* detect lies with certainty;
* understand unknown cultures;
* force agreement;
* override hard interests or doctrine;
* fabricate valid authority;
* create forged credentials;
* negotiate faction-wide treaties;
* determine long-term faction strategy;
* make impossible promises valid;
* turn testimony into truth.

## Tier II — DI2 — Negotiate & Resolve

### Core Promise

> Resolve conflicts where the parties have competing interests, incomplete trust, conflicting obligations, or several linked issues by constructing workable agreements rather than relying on simple persuasion.

Core question:

> **What is actually preventing agreement, what can each side give or accept, and what arrangement can both sides live with?**

Shorthand:

```text
NEGOTIATE / RESOLVE
```

DI2's defining change is:

> DI1 changes an immediate decision. DI2 constructs and manages agreements between parties whose interests do not naturally align.

### DI2 Certification

```text
1. Advanced Diplomatic Examination

8. Mediate Competing Parties
9. Resolve Authority & Obligation Conflict

11. Manage Agreement Breakdown
12. Validate Negotiated Resolution

COMPLETE → DI2
```

### INTEREST_MAPPING_II

Supports identifying the relevant interests of each party in a conflict.

DI1 may identify:

```text
"This supervisor wants the reactor kept safe."
```

DI2 may map:

```text
Supervisor:
- prevent reactor damage
- retain operational authority
- avoid blame
- keep workers safe

SGC:
- inspect reactor
- prevent catastrophic failure
- preserve Haven relationship
- gain access quickly

Governor:
- maintain political control
- avoid public panic
- avoid appearing dependent on SGC
```

Interest maps derive from Evidence.

They do not expose hidden motives automatically.

### POSITION_INTEREST_ANALYSIS_II

Formalizes the distinction:

```text
POSITION
≠
INTEREST
```

Example:

```text
Position:
"No SGC personnel enter the facility."

Interest:
"Local leadership cannot appear to have lost control."
```

Another:

```text
Position:
"We require 100 units of Material."

Interest:
"We need enough resources to keep life support operating until the next shipment."
```

DI2 searches for solutions that satisfy the interest without necessarily accepting the original position exactly.

### CONSTRAINT_IDENTIFICATION_II

Supports determining what a party genuinely cannot or will not accept.

Potential constraints include:

```text
legal restriction
faction doctrine
resource scarcity
security requirement
cultural rule
political exposure
time limit
existing treaty
personal authority limit
physical impossibility
third-party obligation
```

Negotiation should not assume every issue is tradable.

### Hard Constraint vs Preference

DI2 should distinguish:

```text
PREFERENCE
```

from:

```text
HARD_CONSTRAINT
```

where Evidence supports the difference.

Example:

```text
"They would rather not let us inspect the archive."
```

is not the same as:

```text
"The Princess is legally prohibited from disclosing those records."
```

A higher Influence roll does not erase a hard semantic constraint.

### LEVERAGE_ASSESSMENT_II

Supports determining what facts, resources, capabilities, or alternatives materially affect another party's choices.

Potential leverage includes:

```text
needed resource
valuable information
access
protection
transport
medical assistance
political support
Evidence
time pressure
alternative supplier
custody of property
ability to solve shared problem
```

Leverage does not automatically equal coercion.

### Leverage Must Be Real

A Diplomat cannot invent leverage because Influence is high.

```text
"We can provide power cells."
```

only matters if the SGC can credibly provide them.

Diplomatic promises should remain transactionally valid.

### ALTERNATIVE_ASSESSMENT_II

Supports assessing what each side can do if no agreement is reached.

Examples:

```text
SGC can leave
Haven can deny access
SGC can seek governor intervention
Haven can seek CLP assistance
both can wait
SGC could attempt unauthorized entry
```

This asks:

> What happens if no agreement is reached?

That changes bargaining power.

### Alternatives vs Threats

Important distinction:

```text
ALTERNATIVE
≠
THREAT
```

Example:

```text
"If we cannot buy food here, we can trade with another Haven."
```

is an alternative.

```text
"If you do not sell us food, we will seize it."
```

is a threat.

The social consequences differ sharply.

### NEGOTIATION_STRUCTURING_II

Supports breaking complicated disputes into negotiable issues.

Example:

```text
SGC wants:
- access to ruins
- custody of artifact
- armed escort

Haven wants:
- ownership recognized
- local observers present
- compensation
- no permanent SGC presence
```

DI2 may structure:

```text
ACCESS
CUSTODY
SECURITY
COMPENSATION
OBSERVATION
DURATION
```

Diplomacy therefore need not collapse into one persuasion check.

### CONCESSION_MANAGEMENT_II

Supports understanding what each side can give up, under what conditions, and at what cost.

Possible concessions include:

```text
reduced payment
limited access
shorter duration
shared custody
supervised inspection
delayed enforcement
public recognition
information disclosure
security limitation
resource contribution
```

A concession should alter real proposed terms.

Avoid abstract negotiation-progress counters as the primary effect.

### TRADEOFF_ANALYSIS_II

Supports identifying exchanges where parties value terms differently.

Example:

```text
Haven values:
formal recognition of artifact ownership

SGC values:
temporary Research access

Possible trade:
Haven retains ownership
SGC receives 48-hour supervised analysis access
```

Multi-issue bargaining can create real obligations.

### MEDIATION_II

Supports helping two or more other parties reach voluntary agreement.

The Diplomat may:

```text
clarify claims
separate issues
identify shared interest
restate disputed terms
propose neutral procedure
identify acceptable compromise
confirm agreement
```

The Diplomat does not automatically have authority to decide for either party.

### Mediation vs Arbitration

Critical distinction:

```text
MEDIATION
=
help parties reach voluntary agreement

ARBITRATION
=
authorized third party issues binding decision
```

Arbitration requires actual authority from:

```text
agreement
law
faction practice
mission context
```

### OBLIGATION_ANALYSIS_II

Supports understanding how existing promises, treaties, debts, duties, and agreements constrain current choices.

Potential obligations include:

```text
trade contract
mutual defense agreement
custody agreement
prior promise
medical duty
faction policy
debt
temporary ceasefire
inspection agreement
resource commitment
```

A party may want to cooperate while being constrained by existing commitments.

### AUTHORITY_CONFLICT_ANALYSIS_II

Supports identifying overlapping or conflicting authority.

Examples:

```text
Governor authorizes access.
Security Chief refuses entry.

Princess authorizes medical intervention.
Governor disputes jurisdiction.

Facility owner grants access.
Faction law forbids artifact removal.
```

DI2 can identify:

```text
who controls which decision
where authority overlaps
where authority conflicts
what additional approval is required
```

It does not invent a legal answer when subject Theory does not provide one.

### COMMITMENT_DESIGN_II

Supports constructing terms that make future promises credible.

Possible mechanisms include:

```text
upfront exchange
staged performance
escrow
shared custody
inspection rights
deposit
mutual obligation
automatic termination condition
third-party witness
scheduled review
```

Example:

```text
Haven worries SGC will take artifact and never return it.

Agreement:
- artifact remains Haven property
- SGC transports it to base
- Haven observer accompanies it
- analysis limited to 48 hours
- destructive Analysis prohibited
- artifact returned afterward
```

The structure addresses the trust problem.

### Credibility

Promise credibility may depend on:

```text
past behavior
ability to perform
authority to promise
cost of fulfillment
verification
agreement structure
current relationship
```

A promise the SGC cannot perform remains weak even with high Influence.

### Promises Need Authority

A Diplomat cannot validly promise:

```text
"CLP will withdraw all troops from this sector."
```

without authority or a real CLP commitment.

Potential finding:

```text
PROMISE_OUTSIDE_AUTHORITY
```

### AGREEMENT_BREAKDOWN_MANAGEMENT_II

Supports managing agreements that are failing or disputed.

Potential causes include:

```text
missed obligation
ambiguous term
unexpected event
authority change
third-party interference
resource unavailability
alleged breach
new information
```

Possible responses include:

```text
clarify term
renegotiate obligation
grant extension
substitute performance
acknowledge breach
seek remedy
suspend part of agreement
prevent total collapse
```

### NEGOTIATED_RESOLUTION_VALIDATION_II

Supports determining whether negotiation produced a workable resolution.

Possible outcomes include:

```text
FULL_AGREEMENT
PARTIAL_AGREEMENT
TEMPORARY_AGREEMENT
ISSUE_DEFERRED
NEGOTIATION_FAILED
AGREEMENT_UNWORKABLE
AUTHORITY_INSUFFICIENT
```

A negotiation can be successful without resolving every issue permanently.

### DI2 Knowledge Authority

DI2 normally originates:

```text
OBSERVATION
EVIDENCE
INTERPRETATION
QUESTION
INSTANCE_FINDING
```

and structured operational objects such as:

```text
NEGOTIATION

```

Diplomat-flavored findings may include:


### DI2 Generalization Boundary

Repeated negotiation patterns may raise:

```text
GENERALIZATION_QUESTION
```

but DI2 should not automatically create:

```text
Culture Theory

```

from anecdotal interactions.

### DI2 and Deception

DI2 Bluff can support more sophisticated social deception such as:

```text
credible cover story
false negotiating position
concealed reservation value
concealed fallback alternative
strategic ambiguity
```

The same hard rule remains:

```text
Bluff
≠
Reality
≠
Authority
≠

```

### DI2 Capability Boundary

DI2 can:

* map several parties' interests;
* identify hard constraints;
* distinguish positions from interests;
* assess leverage;
* assess alternatives;
* structure multi-issue negotiations;
* manage concessions;
* identify tradeoffs;
* mediate disputes;
* analyze obligations;
* reason about overlapping authority;
* construct credible commitments;
* manage partial agreements;
* renegotiate failing agreements;
* distinguish breach from dispute;
* validate whether a resolution is workable.

DI2 does not inherently:

* force compromise;
* make impossible resources available;
* override faction doctrine;
* invalidate obligations;
* grant arbitration authority;
* guarantee good faith;
* guarantee future compliance;
* make false promises valid;
* create faction-wide policy;
* automatically improve reputation.

## Tier III — DI3 — Align & Represent

### Core Promise

> Represent the SGC in high-stakes political relationships, reconcile strategic interests across Havens or factions, define what a durable diplomatic arrangement requires, and maintain alignment across multiple commitments.

Core question:

> **What larger political relationship is at stake, what must remain true for that relationship to function, and how should the SGC position itself?**

Shorthand:

```text
ALIGN / REPRESENT
```

DI3's defining change is:

> DI3 stops thinking only about one negotiation and starts managing how multiple agreements, obligations, interests, authorities, and political relationships fit together over time.

### INSTITUTIONAL_REPRESENTATION_III

```text

≠
```

### COMMITMENT_RECONCILIATION_III

Supports identifying and managing conflicts between several existing SGC commitments.

Example:

```text

SGC promises Scion Haven exclusive Research access.

Agreement B:
SGC promises CLP equal access to discoveries from shared expedition.
```

```text

```

before accidental breach.

Possible responses include:

```text

limit scope
seek consent

withdraw from one agreement
defer new commitment
```

Preferred sequence:

```text

↓
conflicts surfaced
↓
player decides knowingly
```

This is the diplomatic equivalent of transaction validation.

## Cross-Profession Boundaries

### Soldier Boundary

Soldier may establish:

```text
Opposing force is armed but not advancing.
```

Diplomat may establish:

```text
They are willing to talk if SGC lowers weapons first.
```

Soldier understands immediate threat and engagement state.

Diplomat understands communication, terms, and voluntary social decision.

### Scout Boundary

Scout may establish:

```text
Two guards moved toward the west entrance.
```

Diplomat may establish:

```text
The governor stopped speaking after the guard movement.
```

Scout interprets spatial evidence.

Diplomat interprets social significance.

### Technician Boundary

Diplomat may establish:

```text
The supervisor refuses access because they fear further damage.
```

Technician may establish:

```text
The system is likely to fail without immediate intervention.
```

The two findings can compose into a persuasive argument.

### Scientist Boundary

Diplomat may establish:

```text
This Haven repeatedly describes the phenomenon as sacred.
```

Scientist may establish:

```text
The phenomenon is a measurable energy interaction.
```

One concerns social meaning.

The other concerns physical explanation.

Neither replaces the other.

### Medic Boundary

Diplomat may negotiate:

```text
medical access
patient transfer
resource exchange
quarantine cooperation
```

Medic determines:

```text
clinical need
treatment requirement
patient risk
```

Diplomat cannot invent medical necessity.

Medic cannot automatically create political permission.

## Specialization Boundary

Base Diplomat I–III establishes strong ordinary competency in:

```text
communication
persuasion
bluff
negotiation
mediation
institutional representation
```

Future specializations should own exceptional or narrower forms of social power rather than merely duplicating the base progression.

The base Profession should remain capable without consuming future specialization space.

## Final Profession Standard

A Diplomat should never function as:

```text
high Influence
=
other people do what I want
```

The Profession should create progressively broader social and political reasoning:

```text
DI1:
What does this person want,
and what can change the immediate decision?

DI2:
What prevents agreement,
and what arrangement can reconcile the parties?

DI3:
What larger political relationship is at stake,
and what must remain true for it to function?
```

The Diplomat's power is therefore:

```text
better communication
+
better social interpretation
+
better agreement construction
+
better commitment management
+
better political alignment decisions
```

not control over another Actor's will.
