## Profession Identity

**Code:** `SC`

Scientist progression is centered on increasingly powerful ways of producing and validating knowledge.

```text
SC1 — Observe & Analyze
SC2 — Model & Test
SC3 — Explain & Generalize
```

The progression is:

```text
SC1
What does the evidence show?

SC2
Why is it behaving this way?

SC3
What general truth explains it, and what follows from it?
```

Scientist Curriculum provides **method**.

Subject Theory provides **domain knowledge**.

```text
Scientist Curriculum = scientific method
Subject Theory = what the SGC actually knows about the subject
```

A high-tier Scientist does not automatically understand unknown alien physics, biology, materials, or technology.

## Core Profession Boundary

The boundary is:

```text
Scientist:
What is true?
Why does it happen?
What conditions govern it?
What must be true for the phenomenon to work?

Technician:
How do we physically realize those known conditions?
How do we build, repair, adapt, integrate, or implement the system?
```

Example:

```text
SC3:
Stable containment requires a defined geometry,
energy density, phase relationship, and thermal ceiling.

TE3:
Given those requirements, determine and build
a physical implementation that satisfies them.
```

Scientific Requirements are therefore separate from:

```text
Blueprints
Implementation Designs
Recipes
```

`SC0` means the Unit has committed to the Scientist Curriculum but has not yet demonstrated Tier I competency.

Certification uses **Strenuous** Stamina.

## Tier I — SC1 — Observe & Analyze

### Core Question

> **What does the evidence show?**

SC1 establishes reliable scientific observation, measurement, evidence handling, comparison, classification, interpretation, and verification.

SC1 works primarily within already-established Theory.

---

### SC1 Certification

```text
1. Scientific Foundations Examination
2. Commission Scientist Tools I
3. Controlled Observation
4. Scientific Measurement
5. Evidence Collection & Preservation
6. Comparative Analysis
7. Classification & Interpretation
8. Findings & Verification

COMPLETE → SC1
```

These are semantic certification stages, not necessarily literal one-to-one Recipe bubbles.

---


---

### SCIENTIFIC_SAFETY_I

Supports ordinary scientific safety when the relevant hazard is understood.

Includes:

* recognizing known scientific hazards;
* identifying contamination risk;
* following established sample-handling rules;
* recognizing unsafe analytical conditions;
* selecting ordinary protective precautions;
* recognizing when available containment is inadequate.

It does not make an unknown alien object automatically safe or understood.

A valid SC1 conclusion may be:

```text
Hazard characteristics unknown.
Safe handling requirements cannot yet be established.
```

---

### CONTROLLED_OBSERVATION_I

Supports:

* systematic observation;
* separating observation from assumption;
* recording environmental conditions;
* repeating observations;
* recognizing transient versus persistent behavior;
* identifying potentially relevant variables;
* recognizing obvious observational interference.

Example:

```text
Weak:
"The device is unstable."

Controlled observation:
"Output amplitude oscillates repeatedly while input power remains approximately constant."
```

---

### SCIENTIFIC_MEASUREMENT_I

Supports:

* selecting established measurement methods;
* operating ordinary scientific instruments;
* choosing appropriate ranges;
* recording units;
* repeating measurements;
* recognizing obvious saturation or measurement error;
* documenting ordinary uncertainty.

Subject Theory determines which measurements are meaningful.

Scientist skill does not substitute for subject knowledge.

---

### EVIDENCE_HANDLING_I

Supports:

* preserving samples;
* maintaining provenance;
* recording collection conditions;
* maintaining ordinary chain of custody;
* avoiding ordinary contamination;
* distinguishing raw Evidence from later interpretation;
* identifying obviously compromised Evidence.

Conceptually:

```text
Observation
↓
Evidence Handling
↓
Persistent Evidence Record
```

Not every casual observation requires formal Evidence handling.

---

### COMPARATIVE_ANALYSIS_I

Supports:

* compare Evidence to known baseline;
* compare multiple samples;
* identify similarities and differences;
* compare observed and expected values;
* identify known-range violations;
* compare before and after states.

Example:

```text
Known range:
8.2–8.7

Observed:
9.4

SC1 finding:
Observed output is outside the established operating range.
```

SC1 does not necessarily know why.

---

### SCIENTIFIC_CLASSIFICATION_I

Supports classification using existing institutional knowledge.

Examples:

```text
BIOLOGICAL
MINERAL
MANUFACTURED
ENERGY_EMITTING
KNOWN_MATERIAL_CLASS
KNOWN_PHENOMENON_FAMILY
```

Classification may remain broad.

A valid result is:

```text
MANUFACTURED_DEVICE
ENERGY_EMITTING
UNKNOWN_FUNCTION
```

rather than falsely identifying an unknown mechanism.

---

### KNOWN_MODEL_APPLICATION_I

Supports applying established Theory to Evidence.

Includes:

* using known relationships;
* calculating expected results;
* comparing observation against Theory;
* recognizing model boundary conditions;
* determining whether observed behavior matches expected behavior.

Possible conclusion:

```text
Observed behavior is inconsistent with the established model under recorded conditions.
```

SC1 does not need to explain why.

---

### ANOMALY_IDENTIFICATION_I

Supports recognizing:

```text
Unexpected measurement
Conflicting observation
Missing expected effect
Unexplained variation
Model conflict
Possible unknown variable
```

SC1 can identify:

> Something here requires explanation.

SC1 does not automatically provide the new explanation.

That is the transition toward SC2.

---

### DATA_INTERPRETATION_I

Supports bounded conclusions directly justified by Evidence and established Theory.

Common result states:

```text
SUPPORTED
WEAKLY_SUPPORTED
INCONCLUSIVE
CONTRADICTED
```

SC1 should generally avoid claiming strong falsification of complex models.

---

### FINDINGS_DOCUMENTATION_I

Supports:

* recording methods;
* referencing Evidence;
* recording applied Theory;
* separating observation from interpretation;
* recording uncertainty;
* recording limitations;
* preserving provenance.

Knowledge recording still depends on universal database Read/Write Services.

Physical work can occur during a recording outage, but institutional Knowledge may require later reconciliation.

---

### ANALYTICAL_VERIFICATION_I

Supports analytical quality control.

Includes:

* repeat measurement;
* verify calculations;
* compare observations;
* verify sample identity;
* identify procedural errors;
* confirm that conclusions actually follow from Evidence.

This is not full reproducibility testing.

That belongs to SC2.

---

### SC1 Knowledge Authority

SC1 can normally originate:

```text
OBSERVATION
EVIDENCE
INTERPRETATION
QUESTION
INSTANCE_FINDING
```

SC1 may contribute to Discovery.

SC1 does not normally originate, through SC1 competency alone:

```text
HYPOTHESIS
PREDICTION
WORKING_MODEL
GENERALIZED_FINDING
SCIENTIFIC_REQUIREMENTS
THESIS
THEORY
```

---

### SC1 Boundary

```text
SC1 — OBSERVE / ANALYZE

"Tell me what the evidence shows."
```

SC1 can:

* observe;
* measure;
* preserve Evidence;
* classify;
* compare;
* apply known models;
* identify anomalies;
* interpret established relationships;
* create bounded Instance Findings;
* document and verify findings.

SC1 does not inherently:

* establish novel causality;
* construct new explanatory models;
* design sophisticated discriminating experiments;
* generalize into new scientific principles;
* create Theory.

## Tier II — SC2 — Model & Test

### Core Question

> **Why is it behaving this way?**

SC2 moves from passive analysis to deliberate scientific investigation.

Its defining capability is:

> **SC2 deliberately manipulates conditions to generate new Evidence capable of distinguishing competing explanations.**

---

### SC2 Certification

```text
1. Advanced Scientific Examination
2. Commission Scientist Tools II
3. Characterize Unfamiliar Phenomenon
4. Form Competing Hypotheses
5. Design Controlled Experiment
6. Establish Experimental Controls
7. Test Predicted Relationship
8. Evaluate Causal Relationship
9. Characterize Operating Range
10. Analyze Conflicting Evidence
11. Refine Explanatory Model
12. Assess Reproducibility

COMPLETE → SC2
```

---

### SC2 Competencies

```text
PHENOMENON_CHARACTERIZATION_II
HYPOTHESIS_FORMATION_II

EXPERIMENT_DESIGN_II
VARIABLE_CONTROL_II
PREDICTION_TESTING_II

CAUSAL_ANALYSIS_II
MODEL_DISCRIMINATION_II

OPERATING_RANGE_CHARACTERIZATION_II
CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE_ANALYSIS_II
MODEL_REFINEMENT_II
REPRODUCIBILITY_ASSESSMENT_II
```

SC2 retains all SC1 competencies.

---

### PHENOMENON_CHARACTERIZATION_II

Supports describing unfamiliar but investigable behavior.

Includes:

* identify relevant measurable dimensions;
* vary conditions;
* map responses;
* identify thresholds;
* identify correlations;
* identify stable and unstable regions;
* characterize repeating patterns;
* identify environmental dependencies.

Example:

```text
SC1:
The field oscillates every 4.2 seconds.

SC2:
Oscillation period changes with emitter separation,
but remains unchanged across the tested input-power range.
```

This can produce useful Knowledge even when the underlying mechanism remains unknown.

---

### HYPOTHESIS_FORMATION_II

Supports creating testable candidate explanations.

A hypothesis must be grounded in:

```text
Evidence
+
Known Theory
+
Plausible unresolved relationships
```

Example:

```text
H1:
Thermal feedback causes instability.

H2:
Emitter phase interaction causes instability.

H3:
Power regulation causes periodic instability.
```

Hypotheses are Knowledge Records.

They do not become instance Reality tags.

Multiple competing Hypotheses may coexist.

---

### EXPERIMENT_DESIGN_II

Supports:

* define scientific question;
* determine manipulated variable;
* determine measured result;
* establish controls;
* determine required samples;
* determine necessary Tool Services;
* determine necessary Room Services;
* establish safe test conditions;
* determine the semantic procedure.

Important distinction:

```text
Experiment
≠
Recipe
```

The Scientist defines what the experiment must scientifically accomplish.

The Recipe system determines how the work is executed.

If new apparatus is required:

```text
SC2:
defines experimental conditions.

TE3:
may design the apparatus needed to produce them.
```

---

### VARIABLE_CONTROL_II

Supports:

* identify confounding variables;
* hold relevant conditions constant;
* establish comparison conditions;
* isolate variables where possible;
* recognize uncontrolled interference;
* determine when a result cannot support the intended conclusion.

A scientifically unsuccessful test may still produce useful Knowledge.

Example:

```text
Temperature changed,
but input power changed too.

Result:
Test cannot establish temperature as causal.
```

---

### PREDICTION_TESTING_II

Supports the chain:

```text
Hypothesis
↓
Prediction
↓
Test
↓
Evidence
```

Example:

```text
Hypothesis:
Thermal feedback causes oscillation.

Prediction:
Cooling the system should reduce oscillation.

Test:
Reduce temperature while holding other known variables stable.

Observation:
Oscillation unchanged.

Result:
Prediction contradicted.
```

---

### CAUSAL_ANALYSIS_II

Supports determining bounded causal relationships.

Includes:

* distinguish correlation from causation;
* manipulate suspected causal variables;
* compare controlled conditions;
* establish necessary relationships where supported;
* establish sufficient relationships where justified;
* reject tested alternative causes;
* identify remaining causal ambiguity.

Scope remains important.

```text
A caused B under tested conditions
```

does not mean:

```text
A universally causes B.
```

---

### MODEL_DISCRIMINATION_II

SC2's defining reasoning competency.

Given competing explanations, SC2 asks:

> **What observation would make these explanations behave differently?**

Example:

```text
H1 predicts:
Cooling stops oscillation.

H2 predicts:
Phase adjustment changes oscillation frequency.

H3 predicts:
Oscillation follows regulator load.
```

This can create meaningful choices among different tests, risks, costs, and required infrastructure.

---

### OPERATING_RANGE_CHARACTERIZATION_II

Supports determining:

* thresholds;
* stable ranges;
* instability boundaries;
* environmental dependencies;
* useful operating regions;
* failure onset;
* response curves.

Example:

```text
Stable:
310–470 K

Oscillation begins:
> 470 K

Containment collapses:
> 520 K
```

This Knowledge can become useful to Technician even before the scientific mechanism is fully understood.

---

### CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE_ANALYSIS_II

Supports identifying possible explanations for conflicting Evidence:

```text
Measurement error
Contamination
Uncontrolled variable
Hidden variable
Invalid assumption
Multiple mechanisms
Insufficient sample
Wrong Theory application
Actual anomaly
```

Valid outcome:

> Current Evidence cannot distinguish these explanations.

---

### MODEL_REFINEMENT_II

Supports revising Working Models when Evidence changes.

Includes:

* add boundary conditions;
* remove unsupported assumptions;
* incorporate validated relationships;
* narrow scope;
* distinguish operating regimes;
* revise predictions.

Working Model remains provisional.

It does not automatically modify Theory.

---

### REPRODUCIBILITY_ASSESSMENT_II

Supports determining whether a result persists when relevant conditions are recreated.

Includes:

* repeat experiment;
* compare independent runs;
* compare equivalent samples;
* recreate controlled conditions;
* identify sample-specific effects;
* distinguish stable behavior from one-time anomaly.

Boundary:

```text
SC1:
Did we perform and interpret this analysis correctly?

SC2:
Does the phenomenon happen again when we recreate the conditions?
```

---

### SC2 Knowledge Authority

SC2 can normally originate:

```text
OBSERVATION
EVIDENCE
INTERPRETATION
QUESTION
INSTANCE_FINDING

HYPOTHESIS
PREDICTION
TEST
WORKING_MODEL
```

SC2 can create bounded causal Instance Findings.

SC2 does not normally originate through Curriculum alone:

```text
GENERALIZED_FINDING
SCIENTIFIC_REQUIREMENTS
THESIS
THEORY
```

SC2 may identify a:

```text
GENERALIZATION_QUESTION
```

when several findings appear likely to apply more broadly.

---

### SC2 Boundary

```text
SC2 — MODEL / TEST

"Explain the behavior, then try to prove yourself wrong."
```

SC2 can:

* characterize unfamiliar phenomena;
* create hypotheses;
* derive predictions;
* design controlled experiments;
* manipulate variables;
* investigate causality;
* discriminate models;
* characterize operating ranges;
* analyze conflicting Evidence;
* refine Working Models;
* assess reproducibility.

SC2 does not inherently:

* generalize local findings into broad principles;
* establish institutional Theory;
* design physical implementation;
* understand unknown domains without sufficient knowledge.

## Tier III — SC3 — Explain & Generalize

### Core Question

> **What general truth best explains the evidence, and what follows from it?**

SC3 operates across bodies of Evidence, multiple Working Models, and multiple instances.

It is not simply better experimentation.

Its defining capability is general scientific synthesis.

---

### SC3 Certification

```text
1. Senior Scientific Examination
2. Commission Scientist Tools III
3. Synthesize Multi-Source Evidence
4. Evaluate Competing Models
5. Derive General Scientific Model
6. Establish Boundary Conditions
7. Derive Novel Prediction
8. Design Critical Experiment
9. Analyze Theory Conflict
10. Generalize Instance Findings
11. Derive Scientific Requirements
12. Validate General Explanation

COMPLETE → SC3
```

---

### SC3 Competencies

```text
EVIDENCE_SYNTHESIS_III
MULTI_MODEL_EVALUATION_III

GENERAL_MODEL_DERIVATION_III
BOUNDARY_CONDITION_ANALYSIS_III
NOVEL_PREDICTION_III

SCIENTIFIC_GENERALIZATION_III
SCIENTIFIC_REQUIREMENTS_DERIVATION_III

KNOWLEDGE_GAP_IDENTIFICATION_III
EXPLANATORY_VALIDATION_III
```

SC3 retains all SC1 and SC2 competencies.

---

### EVIDENCE_SYNTHESIS_III

Supports combining:

* multiple experiments;
* multiple samples;
* field observations;
* laboratory Evidence;
* logs;
* prior Instance Findings;
* conflicting Evidence;
* different environmental conditions.

SC3 distinguishes genuinely independent Evidence from repeated copies of the same underlying observation.

---

### MULTI_MODEL_EVALUATION_III

Supports comparing complete explanatory models.

SC3 evaluates:

* explanatory coverage;
* unsupported assumptions;
* predictive success;
* boundary conditions;
* contradictions;
* unexplained Evidence;
* whether multiple mechanisms are required.

Boundary:

```text
SC2:
Which hypothesis survives this test?

SC3:
Which broader model best explains the accumulated body of Evidence?
```

---

### GENERAL_MODEL_DERIVATION_III

Supports producing generalized scientific models from sufficient Evidence and known Theory.

Includes:

* identify repeated relationships;
* isolate general mechanism;
* distinguish incidental instance state;
* identify contributing Theories;
* define assumptions;
* define scope;
* derive candidate generalized model.

Example:

```text
Thermal expansion
→ emitter geometry changes
→ phase relationship shifts
→ secondary field coupling increases
→ regulation becomes unstable
```

The result may be:

```text
GENERALIZED_MODEL_CANDIDATE
```

It is not automatically Theory.

---

### BOUNDARY_CONDITION_ANALYSIS_III

Supports defining exactly where a model applies.

Includes:

* parameter ranges;
* environmental limits;
* device or organism classes;
* threshold transitions;
* multiple operating regimes;
* known exceptions;
* conditions under which another Theory becomes relevant.

This prevents accidental universalization.

---

### NOVEL_PREDICTION_III

Supports deriving a prediction not directly observed before.

Example:

```text
If field coupling is the real mechanism,
changing emitter spacing should shift the instability threshold
even if temperature remains constant.
```

Novel Predictions can create:

* experiments;
* field tests;
* mission objectives;
* hazard warnings;
* requests for samples;
* Analysis goals;
* Discovery opportunities.

---

### CRITICAL_EXPERIMENT_DESIGN_III

Supports designing experiments specifically intended to decide between major competing models.

Includes:

* identify decisive variable;
* derive mutually exclusive predictions;
* minimize ambiguous outcomes;
* determine falsifying results;
* determine required precision;
* determine required environment;
* decide whether destructive risk is justified.

If new apparatus is needed:

```text
SC3:
defines the critical scientific conditions.

TE3:
designs the apparatus that creates them.
```

---

### THEORY_CONFLICT_ANALYSIS_III

Supports determining why Evidence conflicts with established Theory.

Possible explanations:

```text
BAD_EVIDENCE
WRONG_SCOPE
HIDDEN_VARIABLE
MULTIPLE_MECHANISMS
THEORY_INCOMPLETE
THEORY_INCORRECT
UNKNOWN_INTERACTION
```

SC3 should distinguish:

```text
"The Theory is wrong."
```

from:

```text
"The Theory was applied outside its valid boundary."
```

Theory conflict is a major source of Discovery.

---

### SCIENTIFIC_GENERALIZATION_III

Supports determining whether Instance Findings can defensibly apply to a wider scope.

Possible progression:

```text
INSTANCE FINDING
↓
Repeated comparable findings
↓
Shared mechanism identified
↓
Generalization
↓
GENERALIZED FINDING
```

Generalization must account for:

* comparable samples;
* repeated behavior;
* instance-specific causes;
* exceptions;
* scope;
* boundary conditions.

---

### SCIENTIFIC_REQUIREMENTS_DERIVATION_III

The primary Scientist-to-Technician handoff.

Supports translating scientific understanding into conditions that must be satisfied.

Example:

```text
Desired capability:
Stable containment

Requirements:
Field geometry = X
Energy density >= Y
Phase offset = 27–31°
Thermal load < 470 K
Response latency < Z
```

SC3 determines:

```text
WHAT CONDITIONS MUST BE TRUE
```

TE3 determines:

```text
HOW TO PHYSICALLY MAKE THEM TRUE
```

Scientific Requirements are Knowledge.

They are not:

```text
Blueprints
Recipes
Implementation Designs
```

---

### KNOWLEDGE_GAP_IDENTIFICATION_III

Supports identifying exactly what current understanding does not explain.

Example:

```text
Known:
Energy source
Field geometry
Thermal behavior

Unknown:
Stabilization mechanism
```

Possible outputs:

```text
OPEN_QUESTION
DISCOVERY_OPPORTUNITY
MISSING_THEORY_DEPENDENCY
SAMPLE_REQUIREMENT
EXPERIMENT_REQUIREMENT
```

SC3 does more than say:

> We do not know.

It identifies what knowledge is missing.

---

### EXPLANATORY_VALIDATION_III

Supports determining whether a generalized explanation is strong enough to support institutional Research.

Possible states:

```text
VALIDATED_FOR_THESIS
PROVISIONAL
INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE
UNRESOLVED_CONFLICT
REQUIRES_ADDITIONAL_TEST
REJECTED
```

`VALIDATED_FOR_THESIS` does not mean Theory has been acquired.

---

## Tools and Room Services

The exact Tool Service names are not yet locked.

The conceptual progression is:

```text
Scientist Tools I
Basic observation, measurement, sampling, and evidence preservation.

Scientist Tools II
Controlled experimentation, advanced measurement,
multi-channel testing, and deliberate manipulation.

Scientist Tools III
High-resolution characterization, critical-test instrumentation,
precision experimental configuration, and validation-quality measurement.
```

A provisional naming family has been discussed:

```text
SCT1 — Observe
SCT2 — Experiment
SCT3 — Characterize
```

These names are not yet locked.

```text

```
