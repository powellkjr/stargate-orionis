# Stargate: The Long Way Home

The Stargate network was built to survive interruptions.

A journey through the network did not always pass directly from one Stargate to another. Some connections were routed through intermediate Gates, creating paths across enormous distances.

The Ancients understood that those paths could occasionally be interrupted.

A planet might pass between two routing points. A stellar body could temporarily obstruct a connection. Astronomical conditions could make an established route unsafe for a brief period.

For those situations, the Ancients created the **Eclipse Protocol**.

If a route was interrupted while a transfer was already in progress, the affected Gates would enter a deadlocked state. They would refuse new connections while preserving the incomplete transfer in their buffers. Once the interrupted route became viable again, the Gates would reconnect, complete the transfer, and return to normal operation.

It was an elegant solution to a temporary problem.

Then SG-12 went home.

Their mission had been routine. They investigated the assigned world, completed their objectives, dialed Earth and stepped through the Stargate.

Somewhere along their route home, the Eclipse Protocol activated.

The network waited for the obstruction to clear.

It didn't.

And SG-12 remained caught between worlds for nearly five hundred years.

## The Waiting

Stargate Command waited.

SG-12 had not been killed, at least as far as anyone could determine. The Gate network indicated that a transfer remained incomplete. Somewhere inside a system built long before humanity understood what a wormhole was, the members of SG-12 were still on their way home.

So the SGC remained operational.

At first, everyone expected the Eclipse Protocol to resolve. Then days became weeks. Weeks became months. Months became years.

The families of SG-12 wrote letters.

One member received letters from their parents. At first they were ordinary pieces of home: family news, stories about neighbors, birthdays and assurances that they would all be together when the Gate finally opened.

The parents grew older.

Eventually, one died.

The surviving parent continued writing alone.

Then those letters stopped too.

Another member of SG-12 had left behind a spouse and several children. The first letters were filled with school years, birthdays and stories about what the kids were doing.

Then money started appearing in the letters.

Things were getting tight. Other SGC personnel were helping. People were pitching in where they could.

Eventually, that wasn't enough.

Years passed. Their spouse began building a new life. Someone else entered it. The children grew up.

Eventually, those letters stopped.

Another member received letters from an ex-wife.

She was furious.

She had always known this job would get them killed. She accused them of getting themselves stuck for attention. As the years passed and maintaining the SGC became increasingly difficult to justify, she blamed them for that too.

This was exactly the sort of ridiculous disaster she had always expected from them.

But she kept writing.

Year after year, the angry letters continued.

They continued as budgets disappeared and personnel were reassigned.

They continued until the SGC itself was about to be sealed.

The final member of SG-12 received almost nothing.

Occasionally, the personnel system automatically generated a birthday message.

Year after year, the same system remembered someone nobody else seemed to.

Eventually, even the automated messages stopped.

## The Last Watch

The Eclipse Protocol did not clear.

Maintaining an entire Stargate program around a Gate that could neither send nor receive normal traffic became impossible to justify.

Departments closed.

Research programs ended.

Personnel were reassigned.

The SGC shrank to a skeleton operation whose primary purpose was simply to wait for four people who might never arrive.

Eventually, even that had to end.

But dismantling the facility created a problem.

As far as anyone knew, SG-12 might still be alive.

Destroying the Stargate or closing the iris could turn a five-hundred-year delay into a death sentence if the interrupted transfer ever resumed.

So humanity made one final accommodation for four people who might never return.

The Stargate would remain.

The SGC would remain around it.

And the iris would remain open.

Instead of sealing the Gate, they sealed the entire facility.

Entrances were welded shut. Access routes were collapsed and buried. The SGC was deliberately placed beyond practical reach from the surface.

If the Stargate never worked again, nobody would need to enter.

If it did work again, Earth would not be left with an unsecured doorway opening directly onto the planet.

The remaining personnel preserved what they could.

Then the last people left Stargate Command.

The mountain was sealed behind them.

## The Long Way Home

Nearly five hundred years after SG-12 stepped through the Stargate, something changed.

Whatever had prevented the Eclipse Protocol from completing finally ended.

The network resumed a transfer it had never abandoned.

The buffers emptied.

SG-12 walked through the Stargate.

For them, almost no time had passed.

They expected technicians, security personnel and the familiar activity of the embarkation room.

Instead, they stepped into darkness.

Their SGC was still there.

But it was dead.

Dust covered abandoned workstations. Systems had failed. Corridors were inaccessible. Entire sections of the facility were without power.

And the way to the surface was gone.

SG-12 had finally made it home.

They were buried alive inside it.

## What Was Left Behind

As SG-12 restores power and reopens sections of the facility, pieces of what happened begin to emerge.

Not all at once.

A personnel archive becomes accessible.

A box of correspondence is recovered.

A damaged server comes back online.

An administrative section is reopened.

Chapter by chapter, SG-12 reconstructs the centuries they missed.

They find the letters.

They watch parents grow old through words written to a child who never came home. They watch children grow up without a parent. They discover relationships that ended and lives that continued.

They learn who waited for them.

Who blamed them.

And who had nobody waiting at all.

Behind those personal histories is another story.

The death of Stargate Command.

They encounter it backwards.

First they discover the final sealing of the facility.

Then the skeleton crews.

The budget cuts.

The reassigned personnel.

The increasingly desperate attempts to understand why the Eclipse Protocol had not resolved.

Eventually, they reach the records created shortly after their disappearance, when everyone still believed SG-12 would walk back through the Gate any minute.

Near the end of that trail is a final message.

It was left by people who knew they would never live to see it delivered.

The message ends with one instruction:

> **If you are reading this, continue the mission.**

## Stargate Command

SG-12 cannot reach the surface.

But they still have a Stargate.

They have equipment.

They have records.

And they have addresses for worlds humanity once visited.

Somewhere beyond those addresses may be the supplies, technology and people they need to restore the buried facility and eventually find a way back to the surface.

So they do the one thing an SG team knows how to do.

They go through the Gate.

The first missions aren't about saving humanity.

They're about surviving.

Power.

Food.

Materials.

Medicine.

Personnel.

Information.

Every successful expedition brings another piece of Stargate Command back to life.

Rooms are reopened. Systems are repaired. New personnel arrive through the Gate. Old relationships with distant settlements are rediscovered, while entirely new ones are formed.

The buried outpost slowly becomes an SGC again.

And eventually, they find a way out.

## Earth

SG-12 has spent years wondering what waits above them.

They have imagined ruins.

War.

Radiation.

A dead planet.

Instead, they emerge onto a world that is alive.

Earth is beautiful.

Forests have reclaimed abandoned ground. Rivers run through landscapes that have not heard machinery in generations. Wildlife flourishes. The air is clean.

The planet survived.

What SG-12 cannot find is humanity.

No communications answer them.

No aircraft cross the sky.

No government responds to their signals.

There are no functioning cities visible from their position and no obvious civilization waiting to welcome home four soldiers who disappeared five centuries earlier.

Perhaps humanity destroyed itself through war.

Perhaps natural disasters and unchecked climate change caused a collapse from which civilization never recovered.

Perhaps humanity abandoned Earth.

Perhaps people still live somewhere on the planet but have no reason to answer signals coming from five-hundred-year-old military infrastructure buried beneath a mountain.

SG-12 does not know.

Earth is healthy.

Earth is thriving.

Earth is silent.

Five hundred years earlier, four people left this planet believing they were part of an organization whose mission was to explore the worlds beyond the Stargate.

Now those worlds may contain the only human civilization they have left.

For the first time in the history of Stargate Command, Earth is the unknown world.

And humanity lies somewhere beyond the Gate.
